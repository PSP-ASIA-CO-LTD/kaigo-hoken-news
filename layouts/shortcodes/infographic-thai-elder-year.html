Overview: one example elder's year
An example older person, not a real one, goes through one year of the NHSO community long-term care scheme: screened, registered, given a care plan, approved, paid for, visited and paid for again at year end. Values marked example are ours, chosen to illustrate; every rule and amount comes from our guides. The NHSO pays a flat 10,442 baht a year per approved care plan, and we found no current rule that splits it by care group, so this is a walk-through, not a calculator.
Source: The NHSO long-term care cycle
Step 1: Screening
The service unit, หน่วยจัดบริการ (nuai chat borikan), screens dependent people, old and new cases, with the Barthel ADL index, ดัชนีบาร์เธลเอดีแอล (datchani Barthel ADL), which scores ten items. A score of 11 or less makes a person a dependent person: 0 to 4 is the bed-bound group, 5 to 11 the home-bound group. Example: our elder scores 6 (an example value), so she is home-bound. One municipal fund's 2016 guideline surveys every July; that is local practice, not a national date.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 1
Step 2: Registration
People screened are registered in the NHSO LTC programme at ltcnew.nhso.go.th. A fact sheet on care managers says the care manager enters each person's details in the NHSO's database, for the claim. The 2023 workflow chart places registration between screening and the care plan.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 2
Step 3: Care plan
The care manager writes the individual care plan, แผนการดูแลรายบุคคล (phaen kan dulae rai bukkhon), in the Department of Health's 3C program, after pulling the list of dependent people from ltcnew, and prints it for the LTC subcommittee and the caregiver. People are placed in four care groups. Example: group 2, which the 2016 annex describes as home-bound with confusion, reviewed by the care manager at least every 3 months, visited by the family-doctor team at least monthly and by a caregiver once a week. The annex gives every group one full assessment and plan a year. Current official group definitions were not found.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 3
Step 4: Approval
The care manager submits the printed plans to the LTC subcommittee, คณะอนุกรรมการ LTC (khana anukammakan LTC). The plans go forward as a project, the subcommittee approves it, and the approval is recorded in ltcnew. A 2019 NHSO board notice says the money goes "according to projects approved by" the subcommittee. One fund's 2016 guideline asked for proposals by August and paid within 15 working days of approval; that is local practice, not a national timetable.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 4
Step 5: The money
The NHSO pays 10,442 baht per dependent person per year, up from 6,000 baht, announced in June 2024, against an approved individual care plan. In the 2023 workflow chart the lump sum goes to the local government, which draws up the agreement and voucher and transfers the money to the service unit. In ltcnew the local government enters each approved plan with the person's ADL score, care group and TAI. Gap: we found no current rule that splits the 10,442 baht by care group, so this diagram does not show how much of it is spent on our example elder.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 5 · Is there a rule?
Step 6: Caregiver visits
The caregiver gives the care set out in the plan, mostly help with daily living, supervised by the care manager. The NHSO's 2025 guideline gives an example timetable: for group 2, one visit a week of 4 standard hours (the 2016 annex says 4 to 8 visits a month). The caregiver works from the plan printed from 3C, and the care manager enters the caregiver's work in the Department of Health's database. Gap: no national form or app for the caregiver's own visit log was found.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 6
Step 7: Caregiver paid
Three routes, and an area may use more than one: (1) a service unit pays at the fund's rate (a 2021 study found 600 baht a month for 1 to 5 cases, 1,500 baht for 6 to 10); (2) the local government hires local care volunteers, อาสาสมัครบริบาลท้องถิ่น (asasamak boriban thongthin), at 5,000 baht a month, or 6,000 with more training; (3) the 2025 economic-stimulus project, in which the caregiver reports monthly, the local government records the month in ltcnew, and the NHSO pays the caregiver, up to 5,000 or 6,000 baht a month. The project ran from 7 July 2025 to 30 September 2026.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 7
Step 8: Year end
The 2023 workflow chart ends with ADL recorded over 12 months of service in ltcnew. When the project ends, the unit responsible reports its results. The 2016 municipal guideline repeats the round each year, with a new survey every July and new proposals by August.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 8

Example elder (not a real person)

This person is an example. We chose a Barthel ADL score of 6 and care group 2 to illustrate the steps. Neither value comes from a real case.

The rules applied to her are real: a score of 11 or less makes a person dependent, and 5 to 11 is the home-bound group, กลุ่มติดบ้าน (klum tit ban).

Group 2 in the 2016 annex is home-bound with confusion. We use that description because current official definitions were not found.

Source: NHSO LTC cycle, steps 1 and 3

The four care groups (2016 annex)

Group 1, home-bound: care manager review at least every 6 months; family-doctor team at least monthly; caregiver 2 to 4 visits a month.

Group 2, home-bound with confusion: review at least every 3 months; team at least monthly; caregiver once a week (4 to 8 a month).

Group 3, bed-bound: review at least every 3 months; team at least monthly; caregiver once a week (4 to 12 a month).

Group 4, bed-bound and possibly seriously ill or at the end of life: review at least monthly; team at least twice a month; caregiver twice a week (8 to 12 a month).

Every group gets one full assessment and plan a year. These are 2016 figures from a rate annex republished by a municipal fund. The NHSO's 2025 guideline still uses numbered groups, including "4.2, near death". Current official definitions were not found.

Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 3

10,442 baht a year

The NHSO pays 10,442 baht per dependent person per year, up from 6,000, announced in June 2024, against an approved individual care plan. It expected about 600,000 people to be covered.

The money goes to the local government, which signs an agreement with the service unit and pays it.

In 2016 a rate annex set yearly ranges by group (for group 2, 3,000 to 6,000 baht), capped the care manager's assessment and plan at 800 baht a person, and paid caregivers 50 baht a visit of at least an hour. We could not confirm whether these still apply.

Gap: we found no current rule that splits the 10,442 baht by care group.

Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 5 · Who pays

Three ways caregivers are paid

Service unit. At the fund's rate; a 2021 study found 600 baht a month for 1 to 5 cases and 1,500 baht for 6 to 10.

Local hire. Local care volunteers, อาสาสมัครบริบาลท้องถิ่น (asasamak boriban thongthin), hired by the local government at 5,000 baht a month, or 6,000 with 50 more hours of training.

2025 stimulus. The NHSO paid caregivers hired by local governments directly, up to 5,000 or 6,000 baht a month, from 7 July 2025 to 30 September 2026. The caregiver reported monthly and the local government recorded each month in ltcnew.

Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 7
