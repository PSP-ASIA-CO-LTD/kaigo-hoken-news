Overview: one round of the NHSO long-term care cycle
Nine steps, from the local government joining the scheme to the yearly report. Two government systems hold the records: the NHSO LTC programme (ltcnew), where people are registered and money is paid, and the Department of Health's 3C program, where care managers write care plans. Approval happens on paper. The plan is written in 3C, printed, approved, and then re-entered by the local government in ltcnew. Use Next to walk through the steps.
Source: The NHSO long-term care cycle · Which system does what
Step 0: the local government joins
The NHSO pays long-term care money through each area's local health security fund, กองทุนหลักประกันสุขภาพในระดับท้องถิ่นหรือพื้นที่ (kongthun lak prakan sukkhaphap nai radap thongthin rue phuenthi). A local government must belong to that fund before it can join the scheme. A fact sheet based on a 2021 study counted about 6,800 of more than 7,700 local governments as members. Care plans are later approved by a local subcommittee, คณะอนุกรรมการ LTC (khana anukammakan LTC, LTC subcommittee). Our sources name no system for this step.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 0
Step 1: Barthel ADL screening
The Barthel ADL index, ดัชนีบาร์เธลเอดีแอล (datchani Barthel ADL), scores ten items. A score of 11 or less makes a person a dependent person, ผู้ที่มีภาวะพึ่งพิง (phu thi mi phawa phuengphing): 0–4 bed-bound, 5–11 home-bound. In the NHSO's 2023 workflow chart the service unit, หน่วยจัดบริการ (nuai chat borikan), screens old and new cases. One municipal fund's 2016 guideline asks for a survey every July on a form called LTC 1. Recorded on paper or local forms.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 1
Step 2: registration in the NHSO LTC programme
People screened are registered in the NHSO's LTC programme at ltcnew.nhso.go.th. A fact sheet on care managers says the care manager enters each person's details in the NHSO's database, for the claim.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 2
Step 3: assessment, care groups and the care plan
The care manager writes the individual care plan, แผนการดูแลรายบุคคล (phaen kan dulae rai bukkhon), and places each person in one of four care groups, กลุ่ม 1–4 (klum 1–4). The plan is written in the Department of Health's 3C program (care manager, caregiver, care plan). The care manager pulls the list of dependent people from the NHSO programme into 3C, writes the plan and prints it, for the LTC subcommittee and for the caregiver. The current official definitions of the groups were not found; the fullest description is a 2016 annex.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 3
Step 4: approval by the LTC subcommittee
The care manager prints the plans from 3C and submits them to the LTC subcommittee. In the 2023 workflow chart the plans go forward as a project, the subcommittee approves it, and the approval is then recorded in the NHSO programme. A 2019 NHSO board notice says the money goes "according to projects approved by" the subcommittee. One fund's 2016 guideline used forms LTC 2 to LTC 5, an August deadline and payment within 15 working days; that is local practice, not a national timetable.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 4
Step 5: the money, and the double entry
The NHSO pays 10,442 baht per dependent person per year (announced June 2024). In ltcnew the local government imports the project, enters the date the subcommittee approved the plans, and adds each approved plan with the person's ADL score, care group and TAI. It then records the approval, draws up the agreement, issues the payment voucher, ฎีกาเบิกจ่าย (dika boek chai), and records the payment to the service unit. A 2021 study found that, because the care-plan system and the NHSO's system are not linked, care managers did the same paperwork twice.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 5 · Which system does what
Step 6: caregiver visits and records
The caregiver gives the care in the plan, supervised by the care manager. The caregiver works from the plan printed from 3C, and the care manager enters the caregivers' work in the Department of Health's database. Under the 2025 stimulus project each caregiver also sends a monthly service report to the local government. Gap: our guide found no national form or app for the caregiver's own visit log in the NHSO scheme.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 6
Step 7: paying caregivers
Three routes: (1) through a service unit at the fund's rate (a 2021 study found 600 baht a month for 1 to 5 cases, 1,500 baht for 6 to 10); (2) local care volunteers, อาสาสมัครบริบาลท้องถิ่น (asasamak boriban thongthin), hired by the local government at 5,000 or 6,000 baht a month; (3) the 2025 economic-stimulus project, run from 7 July 2025 to 30 September 2026, in which the local government uploads the contract, bank-book page and training certificate to ltcnew, accepts each month's work and records it there, and the NHSO pays the caregiver. Only route 3 is recorded in a named system.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 7
Step 8: reporting and the next round
The 2023 workflow chart ends with ADL recorded over 12 months of service in the NHSO programme; when the project ends, the unit responsible reports its results. The 2016 municipal guideline repeats the cycle each year, with a new survey every July and new proposals by August.
Source: NHSO LTC cycle, step 8

Paper and the LTC subcommittee (คณะอนุกรรมการ LTC, khana anukammakan LTC)

Full name in the 2019 NHSO notice: คณะอนุกรรมการสนับสนุนการจัดบริการดูแลระยะยาวสำหรับผู้สูงอายุที่มีภาวะพึ่งพิง (khana anukammakan sanapsanun kan chat borikan dulae raya yao samrap phu sung-ayu thi mi phawa phuengphing).

The fund's money goes "according to projects approved by" the subcommittee. The care manager submits plans printed from 3C; the approval is then recorded in the NHSO programme.

Screening is on paper or local forms. One fund's 2016 guideline used forms LTC 1 to LTC 5.

Under the 2025 stimulus project, caregivers send a monthly service report to the local government. Its format is not stated.

Source: NHSO LTC cycle · Who does what

NHSO LTC programme (ltcnew.nhso.go.th)

The NHSO's web programme for the community long-term care scheme. Steps recorded in it: registration of dependent people; each project with its approved plans (ADL score, care group, TAI); approval, agreement, voucher and payment; 12 months of ADL and project results.

Since July 2025 it also carries the stimulus caregiver project: contracts, bank details and training certificates are uploaded, each month's work is recorded, and the NHSO pays the caregiver directly.

On 10 October 2026 the site showed the FY2024 payment rule (10,442 baht per person per year against an approved plan), a data-entry window of 1 to 15 September 2026, regional tables with a CSV export, and manuals, including ThaiID log-in. It removes people without Universal Coverage rights and people who have died.

No public interface for outside software.

Source: Government systems · Data and IT

Department of Health 3C program

The name stands for its three parts: care manager, caregiver and care plan. It registers care managers and caregivers and holds individual care plans. The Department of Health built it with Maejo University.

Care managers pull the list of dependent people from the NHSO programme, write the plan, and print it for the LTC subcommittee and the caregiver. They also enter the caregivers' work.

The government data catalogue publishes yearly counts of caregivers registered in 3C, by health region and province, from 2021.

No price is mentioned in our sources, and no public interface is published. Details beyond the basic workflow are not verified: the Department's 3C report could not be reopened on 11 October 2026.

Source: Government systems · Data and IT

Double entry between 3C and ltcnew

3C pulls the list of dependent people from the NHSO programme. No link back was found: approved plans are re-entered by the local government in ltcnew.

A 2021 study found that, because the Department of Health's care-plan system and the NHSO's system are not linked, care managers did the same paperwork twice.

The Department of Health and the NHSO agreed in November 2018 to link the two systems' data. On 10 October 2026 Hfocus reported the Department's Director-General as saying a "3C PLUS" platform linked with the NHSO will be built in 2027. That is a news report.

Source: Government systems: how the systems connect · NHSO LTC cycle

Blue Book app (สมุดบันทึกสุขภาพผู้สูงอายุ, samut banthuek sukkhaphap phu sung-ayu, elderly health record book)

A digital version of the printed elderly health record book, from the Department of Health. DOP lists three aims: self-assessment by older people; screening of older people in the community by health staff, volunteers and local-government staff; and reporting data so partner agencies can share it.

The Google Play listing names care managers, caregivers and local care volunteers among its intended users.

App Store, Google Play, the LINE Official Account "@bluebook" and a website. No price is shown on the store listing.

Link with 3C or the NHSO programme: not published.

Smart อสม. (DHSS app for village health volunteers, อาสาสมัครสาธารณสุขประจำหมู่บ้าน, asasamak satharanasuk pracham muban)

DHSS's 2022 manual trains volunteers to use the app to work and report. Its first function is the monthly อสม. 1 report, which replaces a paper form.

Google Play listing: 3.6 stars from about 10,300 reviews, version 2.5.10, updated 18 February 2026. Recent reviews complain that it is slow, crashes and makes reports hard to send.

Most LTC-fund caregivers are village health volunteers who took the caregiver course.

Link with 3C or the NHSO programme: not published.

Thai COC (continuity of care)

A web application for sending patient information from hospital to primary care. Developed by Surin Hospital with the Surin provincial health office and Khon Kaen University's Faculty of Public Health.

Before discharge, hospital staff enter the patient's details and the system sends an automatic LINE alert to staff at the destination. Home visits are recorded in the Thai COC mobile app.

By 10 October 2018: 39 provinces, 4,898 units, 292,155 patients. Free to its users, per the 2018 report. No newer figure found.

Link with 3C or the NHSO programme: not published.

3C PLUS (planned)

On 10 October 2026 Hfocus reported the Department of Health's Director-General as saying the Department will build an online platform, "3C PLUS", linked with the NHSO, in 2027.

That is a news report. Our guides found no Department of Health document on it.

Gap: the caregiver's own visit log

Our guide found no national form or app for the caregiver's own visit log in the NHSO scheme.

What is recorded instead: the caregiver works from the plan printed from 3C; the care manager enters the caregivers' work in the Department of Health's database; under the 2025 stimulus project, caregivers send a monthly report to the local government, which records the month in ltcnew.

Outside the NHSO scheme, DOP's own community caregivers use NSTDA's Nirun for Community app.
