National budget (cabinet framework)

The NHSO community LTC money is a line in the national health security budget. The government release on the cabinet decision of 4 February 2025 is titled as approving an FY2026 framework of 275,000 million baht.

The line "public-health services for dependent people in the community" is 5,514,367,800 baht, up 2,614,121,800 baht (90.13%) on FY2025.

This is the cabinet-approved framework, not the final enacted budget. The final FY2026 allocation was not found.

Source: Who pays

NHSO (สำนักงานหลักประกันสุขภาพแห่งชาติ, Samnakngan Lakprakan Sukkhaphap Haeng Chat, National Health Security Office)

Runs the community long-term care (LTC) scheme inside the Universal Coverage Scheme fund (the "gold card" or "30 baht" scheme). The scheme started under a 2016 government policy and works through local governments.

Pays a lump sum of 10,442 baht per dependent person per year, up from 6,000 baht (NHSO board, announced June 2024). It pays the service unit or the local government that runs the local health security fund, according to the number of people with an approved individual care plan (FY2024 fund notice, clause 74).

About 600,000 people are expected to be covered after the increase, up from about 320,000 a year.

Runs the LTC web programme at ltcnew.nhso.go.th. It holds payment rules, data-entry windows, regional reports and a ThaiID log-in, and it removes people without Universal Coverage rights and people who have died.

A news report says the Department of Health plans an online platform, "3C PLUS", linked with the NHSO, in 2027.

Source: Who pays · Data and IT

Ministry of Interior

Issued the 2023 regulation on paying the old-age allowance, in force from 12 August 2023. Local governments pay the allowance under it.

Three ministries are involved: MSDHS (through DOP and the National Commission on the Elderly), the Ministry of Interior (through local governments, which pay) and the Ministry of Finance.

The same government release said the allowance budget grew from 50,000 million baht a year to 80,000 million, and to 90,000 million baht in FY2024.

Source: Who pays

Residents and families (out-of-pocket)

Private care homes licensed by DHSS charge their own fees. Each home must display its list of services and prices and sign a written service contract with the resident or a representative.

No official national data on prices was found. No NHSO payment to private care homes for residents was found.

Source: Who pays · Licensing

Ministry of Public Health (MOPH)

The Minister of Public Health is in charge of the Health Establishment Act B.E. 2559 (2016). The Minister may issue ministerial regulations, set fees within the Act's schedule and add "other businesses" to the Act (s. 4). A 2020 ministerial regulation added elderly or dependent-person care.

Two MOPH departments appear in this diagram: DHSS, which licenses private care homes, and the Department of Health, which runs the 3C program and the community caregiver course.

Source: Licensing

DHSS (กรมสนับสนุนบริการสุขภาพ, Krom Sanapsanun Borikan Sukkhaphap, Department of Health Service Support; สบส.)

The licensing authority for elderly-care businesses is the Director-General of DHSS (s. 3).

There are three licences: a business licence for the owner (valid five years, annual fee), an operator licence for the manager, and registration for each caregiver. The caregiver certificate form is "สพส.14".

Standards include a client history register, a needs assessment on admission and every three months, and in type 3 homes no more than five clients per caregiver.

Officials may enter and inspect during opening hours (s. 35). Running without a licence carries up to six months in prison, a 50,000 baht fine, or both (s. 42).

1,203 licensed establishments nationwide on 10 October 2026.

Accredits caregiver and operator courses: 159 institutions held DHSS-accredited elderly-care courses on 10 August 2023.

Applications go through the esta system; the citizen guide says electronically only.

Source: Licensing · Caregiver training

Department of Health (กรมอนามัย, Krom Anamai)

Runs the "Long Term Care 3C" program. Its open dataset counts registered caregivers by health region and province. It is updated yearly, from 2021.

Sets the 70-hour community caregiver course (manual revised 2024).

News report: the Director-General said 135,591 caregivers care for 560,461 dependent people in the community. The underlying table was not found.

News report: a "3C PLUS" online platform linked with the NHSO is planned for 2027.

Source: Data and IT · Caregiver training

Ministry of Social Development and Human Security (MSDHS)

The Department of Older Persons is part of MSDHS.

MSDHS is one of three ministries involved in the old-age allowance, through DOP and the National Commission on the Elderly.

MSDHS's three-year plan: four DOP caregivers in each of 7,772 local governments, 31,088 caregivers in all.

NSTDA says data from its Nirun for Community app feeds MSDHS policy-making.

Source: Public and private providers · Nirun

DOP (กรมกิจการผู้สูงอายุ, Krom Kitchakan Phu Sung-ayu, Department of Older Persons)

Set up on 6 March 2015.

Its website lists 12 social welfare development centres for older persons, plus a learning and training centre in Chon Buri. Its statistics table for 28 August 2026 covers 11: Khon Kaen is listed but not in the table.

Runs the Community Elderly Care and Rights Protection Project, whose caregivers use NSTDA's Nirun for Community app.

DOP's centres use NSTDA's Nirun records system.

What residents of each group pay is not stated on the DOP page.

Source: Public and private providers · Nirun

Local governments (องค์กรปกครองส่วนท้องถิ่น, ongkon pokkhrong suan thongthin, local administrative organisations)

The NHSO describes local governments as the hub that links health care with social care in their area.

They receive the LTC lump sum when they run the local health security fund.

2025 economic-stimulus project (NHSO LTC site, 10 Oct 2026): 12,125 caregivers employed; 2,117 local governments with agreements; 738,696,000 baht in pay under the agreements; 11,273 caregivers to continue at 1,877 local governments. The Bangkok region (NHSO region 13) shows no entries.

They pay the monthly old-age allowance.

Source: Who pays · Who pays: old-age allowance

Service units

Under clause 74 of the FY2024 fund notice, the NHSO pays "the service unit or the local government that runs the local health security fund", according to the number of dependent people with an approved individual care plan.

The NHSO LTC programme has separate manuals for service units and for local governments' dependent-elderly funds.

The site does not yet say which bodies count as service units.

Source: Who pays · Data and IT

Approved individual care plan

NHSO money is paid per dependent person with an approved individual care plan, as a lump sum per person per year.

Who qualifies: a Barthel ADL score of 11 or less on ten items (0–4 bed-bound, 5–11 home-bound). In 2024 the NHSO board added people with dementia from the moderate stage and people at the end of life.

The LTC programme lets users edit each person's approved amount.

The site does not yet say who writes or approves the plan.

Source: Who pays

Community caregivers (ผู้ดูแลผู้สูงอายุ, phu dulae phu sung-ayu, caregiver; CG)

Trained on the Department of Health's 70-hour course (manual revised 2024).

Registered in the Long Term Care 3C program.

Stimulus-funded caregivers hired by local governments: 12,125 (11,192 paid 5,000 baht a month, 933 paid 6,000), caring for 106,377 dependent people.

News report: 135,591 caregivers care for 560,461 dependent people.

Source: Caregiver training · Who pays

DOP community caregivers (ผู้บริบาลคุ้มครองสิทธิผู้สูงอายุ, phu boriban khumkhrong sitthi phu sung-ayu; บสส.)

Caregivers in DOP's Community Elderly Care and Rights Protection Project. This is a social-welfare programme, separate from the NHSO scheme.

They complete a 240-hour course. The Bangkok Post reports a monthly stipend of 10,000 baht.

Growth: 2024 pilot, 19 areas and 35 caregivers; 2025, 156 areas in 76 provinces; 2026, 268 areas in 76 provinces, with 587 more caregivers trained.

They record visits in NSTDA's Nirun for Community app.

The number of older people they serve is published inconsistently (34,200 or "more than 342,000"), so it is not shown.

Source: Nirun · Nirun: scale

Dependent older people (ผู้มีภาวะพึ่งพิง, phu mi phawa phuengphing, dependent people)

Qualify for the NHSO scheme with a Barthel ADL score of 11 or less (0–4 bed-bound, 5–11 home-bound). Moderate dementia and end of life were added in 2024.

About 600,000 people a year are expected to be covered after the 2024 increase.

In practice the scheme appears to cover Universal Coverage members only: the NHSO system removes "non UC" people. The site found no rule that says so directly.

Source: Who pays

Older people (aged 60 and over)

14,530,607 Thai nationals aged 60 or over, 22.46%, on 30 September 2026. Of these, 1,583,233 are aged 80–89, 341,220 are aged 90–99 and 47,316 are aged 100 or over.

Old-age allowance (เบี้ยยังชีพผู้สูงอายุ, bia yang chip phu sung-ayu, old-age living allowance), per month: 600 baht at ages 60–69, 700 at 70–79, 800 at 80–89 and 1,000 at 90 and over.

Source: Ageing and market statistics · Who pays

Licensed private care homes (กิจการการดูแลผู้สูงอายุหรือผู้มีภาวะพึ่งพิง, kitchakan kan dulae phu sung-ayu rue phu mi phawa phuengphing, elderly or dependent-person care business)

1,203 licensed nationwide on 10 October 2026. The April 2025 list had 927, 905 of them type 3 (overnight care); Bangkok had 307 and Nonthaburi 112.

Three types: type 1 daytime care, type 2 care with a residence, type 3 care and palliative support with overnight stay.

They must keep an operator on site and employ only registered caregivers, who must be aged 18 or over and hold a DHSS-accredited certificate.

REIC's 2025 survey counted 17,349 nursing-home beds (14,370 occupied) in 944 homes. REIC does not say whether every home is licensed.

Paid by residents and families; no NHSO payment to private homes was found.

Care software sold to these homes

Source: Licensing · Public and private providers

DOP welfare centres (ศูนย์พัฒนาการจัดสวัสดิการสังคมผู้สูงอายุ, sun phatthana kan chat sawatdikan sangkhom phu sung-ayu, social welfare development centre for older persons)

On 28 August 2026: 1,620 target places and 1,547 residents. Waiting lists are 2,149 in the welfare group (กลุ่มสงเคราะห์, klum songkhro) and 6,530 in the self-reliant group (กลุ่มพึ่งพาตนเอง, klum phuengpha ton-eng).

Ban Bang Khae (Bangkok) alone accounts for 7,504 of the 8,679 people waiting.

What each group pays is not stated.

The centres use NSTDA's Nirun records system.

Source: Public and private providers · Nirun

Training schools

159 institutions had DHSS-accredited elderly-care courses on 10 August 2023. There were 223 care schools (โรงเรียนบริบาล, rongrian boriban) under the Office of the Private Education Commission on 28 April 2022. The two counts overlap.

DHSS courses: the 420-hour caregiver course and the 130-hour operator course. OPEC says only higher-education institutions may teach the operator course.

After each cohort, schools must send DHSS the list of graduates and enter the data in its system. DHSS uses this data to register caregivers.

Source: Caregiver training · Data and IT
