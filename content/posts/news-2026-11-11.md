## Highlights

- **Which areas count as "specified".** On 9 November the 138th {{< ja "介護保険部会" "kaigo hoken bukai" "Long-Term Care Insurance Subcommittee" >}} received MHLW's criteria for {{< ja "特定地域" "tokutei chiiki" "specified areas" >}}. These are areas already covered by the special-area add-on or remote-island rules, or areas where people aged 75+ number fewer than 5 per km², or fewer than 1,000 and falling. Areas where existing services can no longer be kept going can also qualify.[^1] Prefectures designate them after asking municipalities, and may do so part-way through the 10th plan period.[^2]
- **A capped funding route.** The {{< ja "特定地域居宅サービス等事業" "tokutei chiiki kyotaku sābisu-tō jigyō" "specified-area home-service project" >}} starts in April 2027.[^4] Municipalities would pay providers project funds within a cap that is still being worked out.[^3]
- **Fee-charging homes register from 1 December 2027.** That is the planned start date, with a one-year transition to 30 November 2028. {{< ja "登録施設介護支援" "tōroku shisetsu kaigo shien" "registered-facility care support" >}} would start on 1 December 2028.[^5]
- **ZEST.** On 10 November ZEST said it had rebuilt its Smart Allocation scheduling engine.[^7]

## 1. The 138th subcommittee (9 Nov)

MHLW's reference count was 356 municipalities (20.5%) that meet the criteria across their whole area. That is 299 already wholly in special-add-on areas, plus 40 that qualify on density and 17 on population.[^1] A cap for the new project would be set separately from other {{< ja "地域支援事業" "chiiki shien jigyō" "community support projects" >}}. It could take account of benefit spending before the project began, the extra costs of remote areas, and changes in the older population.[^3] Applications, review and public listing for registered {{< ja "有料老人ホーム" "yūryō rōjin hōmu" "fee-charging homes for older people" >}} would all be handled in one new system. The public listing would build on an upgraded {{< ja "介護サービス情報公表システム" "kaigo sābisu jōhō kōhyō shisutemu" "care service information publication system" >}}.[^6] These are plans, not final rules. See [private-pay housing]({{< relref "guides/private-pay-elderly-housing" >}}) and [the money and 2027]({{< relref "04-budget-and-outlook" >}}).

## Also this week

The new ZEST engine can book visits that only a few staff can cover first. It can also prefer each user's {{< ja "主担当" "shutantō" "main carer" >}}. ZEST says it is about 20 times faster in its own benchmark, and it is in beta.[^7] See [ZEST]({{< relref "competitors/zest" >}}). SMS is running BCP study sessions nationwide for Kaipoke users.[^8] See [Kaipoke]({{< relref "competitors/kaipoke" >}}).

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: designation can cover only part of a municipality, such as a former town or a ward. Care software would therefore need an area flag below the municipal level. It would also need a third payment route, project contracts with the municipality, alongside per-visit and bundled fees. The registration rules require proof that the care manager is independent and that accounts are kept separately. For housing operators, that is a compliance feature they will want built in. ZEST's rebuild shows that carer continuity is now a basic requirement for scheduling.
