**In three lines**

- Private care homes are licensed by DHSS as "health establishments", under a 2016 Act extended to elderly care from January 2021.
- The law licenses three roles: the business, its operator (manager) and each caregiver, and sets record-keeping standards.
- DHSS counted 1,203 licensed homes in October 2026; the state's 12 welfare centres hold about 1,600 places.

## Three kinds of provider

| Provider | Run by | Scale | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| State welfare centres | DOP, Ministry of Social Development and Human Security | 12 centres; 1,620 target places in the 11 in DOP's August 2026 table | [^1][^2] |
| Licensed private care homes | Private businesses, licensed by DHSS | 1,203 licensed establishments, 10 October 2026 | [^3] |
| Community care at home | Service units and local governments, paid by the NHSO | About 600,000 people expected after the 2024 increase | [^4] |

The Real Estate Information Center's 2025 survey found 1,019 nursing homes, with 17,349 beds available and 14,370 occupied in the 944 that reported beds.[^5] It does not say whether every home holds a DHSS licence, so its count should not be added to the DHSS figure. On these figures private beds outnumber state places roughly ten to one (see [Public and private providers]({{< relref "/thailand/public-and-private-providers" >}})).

## The law

The Health Establishment Act B.E. 2559 (2016), {{< th "พระราชบัญญัติสถานประกอบการเพื่อสุขภาพ" "Phraratchabanyat Sathan Prakop Kan Phuea Sukkhaphap" "Health Establishment Act" >}}, covers spas and massage shops as well as care homes.[^6] A 2020 ministerial regulation added elderly or dependent-person care to the businesses it covers. It took effect 180 days after publication on 31 July 2020; DHSS gives 27 January 2021.[^7][^8] The regulator is DHSS, in the Ministry of Public Health, and the licensing authority is its Director-General or a delegate.[^6] Care inside licensed hospitals and clinics falls under a different law.[^7]

The regulation defines three service types:[^7]

| Type | Service | Overnight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Day care | No |
| 2 | Care with a place to live | Residence provided |
| 3 | Care and palliative support | Yes |

On DOP's April 2025 compilation of DHSS licences, 905 of 927 establishments were type 3, and Bangkok (307) and Nonthaburi (112) held the most.[^9]

## Three licences

The Act separates three roles:[^6]

- **The licensee**, the business, needs a five-year licence and pays an annual fee.
- **The operator**, {{< th "ผู้ดำเนินการ" "phu damnoen kan" "operator" >}}, the manager the licensee must keep on site during opening hours, needs an operator licence, a DHSS-accredited certificate and a DHSS test.
- **Each caregiver**, the service provider, must be registered, aged at least 18, with a DHSS-accredited certificate.

The licensee must keep a register of operators and caregivers and employ only registered caregivers. The operator must record each client's basic health data and screening.[^6] Running without a licence can bring six months in prison, a 50,000-baht fine, or both.[^6] For the courses behind the certificates, see [Caregiver training and schools]({{< relref "/thailand/caregiver-training-and-schools" >}}).

## The standards, and the records they require

The standards regulation covers premises, safety and service.[^10] The service rules that create records are:

- a client history register in a form set by the DHSS Director-General
- an assessment of care needs on admission, repeated **every three months**
- records of changes in each client's health
- a displayed price list and a written service contract
- confidentiality of client data
- for type 3, no more than five clients per caregiver

In October 2026 DHSS said its inspections focus on premises, safety and service, and warned that care homes must not take patients too complex for them or give treatment they have no power to give.[^3]

## Applying

Applications are filed online in DHSS's esta system, which the citizen guide says is the only way to apply for this business type. Officials check the file, visit to assess the premises, and a committee decides; the guide gives two months in all.[^11] Full steps are in [Licensing elderly-care businesses]({{< relref "/thailand/licensing-elderly-care-businesses" >}}).

## Growth

DOP's compilation published in April 2025 listed 927 licensed establishments; DHSS gave 1,203 on 10 October 2026.[^9][^3] The two counts come from different sources and may not be counted the same way, so the difference is only a rough indicator of growth.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: the licensing rules create a ready-made list of records for a care-home product: a staff register, client health data and screening, a client history register, three-monthly needs assessments, change records, price lists and contracts. None of it has to be electronic. Most licensed homes are overnight-care (type 3) businesses concentrated in Bangkok and its neighbours, which is where a care-home product would find most of its customers. The state centres are few, and the community scheme is a different, public market.
