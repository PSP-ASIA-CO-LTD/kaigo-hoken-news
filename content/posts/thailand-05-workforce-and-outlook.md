**In three lines**

- About 13,600 care managers and 87,000 caregivers worked in the NHSO scheme in 2020; the Department of Health now speaks of 135,591 caregivers.
- The official projection has people aged 60 or over rising from 22% of the population in 2026 to 31% in 2040, while the working-age population shrinks by about 5 million.
- Three things are changing at once: a new 3C PLUS platform in 2027, the move of sub-district hospitals to provincial governments, and the question of who pays the stimulus caregivers now the project has ended.

## The community workforce

| Role | Number | Training | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Care manager, {{< th key="phu-chatkan-kan-dulae-phu-sung-ayu" >}} | 13,615 in the NHSO fund (2021 study) | 70-hour course; mostly nurses at sub-district hospitals | [^1] |
| Caregiver, {{< th key="phu-chuai-luea-dulae-phu-sung-ayu" >}} | 86,829 in 2020; 135,591 reported in October 2026 | The Department of Health's 70-hour course | [^1][^2][^3] |
| Village health volunteer, {{< th key="asasamak-satharanasuk-pracham-muban" >}} | Most caregivers come from their ranks | Monthly reports in Smart อสม. | [^4][^5] |

The NHSO fund's target is one care manager for every 5 to 10 caregivers; the 2021 study counted 1 to 6.4, and warned that the target assumes care managers have no other job, which in Thailand they do.[^1] The October 2026 caregiver figure is a news report of a spoken number, and the Director-General gave it with 560,461 dependent people in the community.[^2] See [Who does what in Thai community care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-does-what-community-care" >}}).

## The care-home workforce

Caregivers in licensed care homes need a DHSS-accredited certificate and registration; operators need a certificate and a DHSS test.[^6] DHSS listed 159 institutions with accredited elderly-care courses in August 2023, and the Ministry of Education counted 223 non-formal private "care schools" in 2022.[^7][^8] Course levels range from the 70-hour community course to a 420-hour caregiver standard and a 130-hour operator course (see [Caregiver training and schools]({{< relref "/thailand/caregiver-training-and-schools" >}})).[^9][^10]

## The numbers to 2040

The Office of the National Economic and Social Development Council (NESDC) projects Thailand's population to 2040. The Department of Older Persons republished the projection in its 2023 situation report:[^11]

| Year | Total population | Aged 15 to 59 | Aged 60 or over | Share 60 or over |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026 | 67,143,295 | 41,582,935 | 15,064,349 | 22.44% |
| 2030 | 67,140,539 | 40,105,230 | 17,118,697 | 25.50% |
| 2034 | 66,749,444 | 38,694,668 | 18,750,030 | 28.09% |
| 2040 | 65,372,337 | 36,498,965 | 20,510,045 | 31.37% |

Source for the table: DOP, table 16, citing the NESDC projection.[^11] On this projection the population starts falling after 2028, the working-age group shrinks by about 5.1 million between 2026 and 2040, and people aged 60 or over pass 28% in 2034. In 2040 there would be about 1.8 people aged 15 to 59 for every person aged 60 or over, against about 2.8 in 2026 (our arithmetic from the table).

Two cautions. The projection's 2026 figure, 15.06 million older people, is higher than the 14.53 million Thai nationals aged 60 or over in the house registers on 30 September 2026; the two count different populations.[^12][^11] And a 2022 Ministry of Education press release put the move to a "super-aged society" (over 28% older) in 2031, three years earlier than this table (see [Ageing and market statistics]({{< relref "/thailand/ageing-and-market-statistics" >}})).[^13]

## What's changing

**3C PLUS, 2027.** The Director-General of the Department of Health was reported on 10 October 2026 as saying the Department will build an online platform, "3C PLUS", linked with the NHSO, in 2027, and will train caregivers further as "Super CG".[^14] If it links the two systems, it would address the double entry that a 2021 study found.[^1] No specification has been published that we found.

**Sub-district hospitals move to provinces.** By FY2026, 4,559 sub-district health promoting hospitals, 46.2% of the total, had moved from the Ministry of Public Health to 63 provincial administrative organisations; 47 provincial governments have asked to take over 629 more in 2027.[^15][^16] Staff choose whether to move with their hospital, so a care manager's employer may now be a provincial government.[^15]

**After the stimulus project.** The NHSO's caregiver stimulus project ran from 7 July 2025 to 30 September 2026.[^17] In March 2026, with about 10,000 of its 18,000 target caregivers hired, the NHSO Secretary-General said the NHSO would seek an extension.[^18] On 7 September 2026 the Department of Local Administration told local governments that the NHSO had confirmed support for continued caregiver hiring after the project ended, and asked them to record their needs in the LTC programme by 15 September 2026.[^19] The NHSO programme site showed 11,273 caregivers at 1,877 local governments that local governments want to keep paying.[^20] We found no published amount or rule for this continued funding, so whether it becomes permanent is not yet known.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: Thailand's care workforce grew fast through public programmes, from about 87,000 caregivers in 2020 to the 135,591 the Department of Health now cites, while care managers stayed at a ratio that already stretched them in 2021. The projection says the number of older people will keep rising for 14 years while working-age people fall, so the pressure on each care manager and caregiver will grow. The three changes under way all move the same records: who employs the care manager, which platform holds the care plan, and who pays the caregiver. For a care-software vendor, that means the rules will keep shifting through 2027, and anything built for this market has to follow the government systems as they change.
