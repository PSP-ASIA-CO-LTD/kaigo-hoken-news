CAREKARTE ({{< ja "ケアカルテ" "kea karute" "CAREKARTE" >}}) is care software for long-term care and disability-welfare services. Its vendor says it supports operations from "records and plans to claims".[^1] It is made by Care Connect Japan Co., Ltd. ({{< ja "株式会社ケアコネクトジャパン" "kabushiki-gaisha kea konekuto japan" "Care Connect Japan Co., Ltd." >}}), based in Shizuoka.[^2]

## At a glance

| Item | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Vendor | Care Connect Japan Co., Ltd., Shizuoka; established February 2003; capital ¥197.56 million[^2] |
| Pricing model | Initial fee plus annual maintenance. From the second year only the annual maintenance fee is charged.[^3] No extra renewal fee, and no extra charge for law changes or version upgrades.[^1] |
| Published price | None. The price is quoted after a hearing.[^3] |
| Licences | Under the standard pack, unlimited PC and mobile devices (contract terms may vary)[^1] |
| Customer count | Two figures on the vendor's own site: "about 21,000 offices" on the home page and "about 19,000 offices" on the product page[^4][^1] |
| Share claims | No market-share figure found |
| LIFE | Supports all MHLW LIFE forms and outputs CSV for upload, with an error check[^5] |
| Claims to kokuho-ren | Creates claim data automatically from service records[^1]; transmission method not described in the sources we opened |
| Care Plan Data Exchange | Completed V4 vendor-test patterns D1–D7[^6] |

## Pricing

Care Connect Japan does not publish prices. Its pricing page says CAREKARTE is used under an initial fee plus an annual maintenance fee, and that from the second year only the maintenance fee applies. It compares this with a five-year pack, and says prices are given after a hearing.[^3] The product page adds two points:[^1]

- After the first-year initial fee, there is no further renewal fee.
- Law changes and CAREKARTE version upgrades cost nothing extra.

MHLW's October 2025 function survey lists CAREKARTE's sales model as **annual payment** ({{< ja "年額払い方式" "nengaku-barai hōshiki" "annual payment" >}}).[^7]

## Main features

- **Records to claims.** One system for records, plans and claims. Claim data is created automatically from records of services delivered, and billed and received amounts can be totalled.[^1]
- **Mobile entry.** "CAREKARTE Mobile" for smartphone and tablet entry, which the vendor says takes "2 seconds" per record.[^4] "CAREKARTE Pocket", released in 2026, packs the PC version's functions into a smartphone app.[^4][^8]
- **Voice AI ("Hanasuto").** Staff record care, messages and handovers by speaking, hands-free, with an intercom function. It covers residential and day services and links only to CAREKARTE.[^9] The vendor cites a user survey by ExaWizards showing about **40 minutes** saved per staff member per day. This is a vendor-reported figure.[^9]
- **Home-visit tool ("CAREKARTE Buddy").** Visit records and actuals entry, proof of service using GPS, and chat between helpers. Released in 2022.[^4][^8]
- **Family and partner chat ("Careconne").** A chat app for users, families and people inside and outside the office. Records can be sent as chat messages, and chats can be saved as records.[^4]
- **Management and sensors.** "CAREKARTE Pivot" totals sales forecasts, occupancy, and accident and near-miss figures. "Caremoni" shows records on a large monitor, and "Careposi" tracks the location of users and staff with tags.[^4]
- **Open link interface ("CAREKARTE Link").** Partner manufacturers connect through an API. The Link page lists more than 50 partner products, in categories including:[^10]
  - monitoring sensors, nurse call and vital-sign devices
  - day-service transport and short-stay bookings
  - visit-service systems
  - medication, billing and medical systems

  The visit-service partners include SmaCare and ZEST.[^11][^12]

## Target customers

The product page lists coverage across long-term care and disability welfare:[^1]

- **Residential and facility services:** special nursing homes, geriatric health facilities, care medical facilities, specified facilities, group homes and short stays.
- **Day and visit services:** day care and day rehabilitation; home-visit care, bathing and nursing (medical-insurance nursing included); visiting rehabilitation and 24-hour visiting services.
- **Community and other services:** small-scale multifunctional services, care management, and welfare-equipment rental.
- **Disability welfare:** a wide range of services for adults and children.

The case studies on the product page are mostly social-welfare corporations running facilities.[^1]

## Scale and history: what the company says

All figures in this section are Care Connect Japan's own.

- **Office count.** The home page says CAREKARTE is used by "about 21,000 offices".[^4] The product page says "about 19,000 offices nationwide".[^1] The site does not explain the difference or give a date for either figure.
- **History.** The business started in 1989 as the care-systems division of Suzuyo Warehouse. It became independent through a management buyout in 2003 as Fuji Data System.[^8] Other milestones:[^8]
  - 1996: the facility records system "Chōju" ({{< ja "ちょうじゅ" "chōju" "Chōju" >}}) launched
  - 2018: CAREKARTE launched
  - 2020: the company took its current name and launched CAREKARTE Link
- **Partners.** The company lists two partnerships:[^8]
  - 2022: a business partnership with ExaWizards
  - 2023: a capital and business alliance with Canon System & Support
- **Vietnam.** It set up a local subsidiary in Vietnam in 2019 and supported the founding of a home-visit care business there in 2023.[^8]

## LIFE handling

CAREKARTE says it supports **every** LIFE form specified by MHLW, plus three forms of its own. Data entered in CAREKARTE (or the older Chōju system) fills in the LIFE forms automatically from basic and insurance information and from other records. The forms can be copied in bulk, and they are output as CSV for upload to the LIFE site. Before output, the software checks the data against MHLW's specification and colour-codes any errors.[^5]

## Claims to kokuho-ren

The product page says claim data is created automatically from the records of services delivered.[^1] The sources we opened do not say whether CAREKARTE transmits claims to the federation ({{< ja "国保連" "kokuho-ren" "prefectural National Health Insurance federation" >}}) itself. In MHLW's October 2025 function survey, CAREKARTE's entry marks creation of claim statements and invoices for visit, day, residential and facility services. It does not mark claim transmission for any service type.[^7]

## Care Plan Data Exchange

Kokuho Chūōkai's V4 vendor-test list shows CAREKARTE as having completed all seven patterns, D1–D7.[^6] In MHLW's October 2025 survey, Care Connect Japan reported two things:[^7]

- a completed vendor test for standard version 3.2;
- planned support for version 4.1.

The entry lists the care-plan tables and provision-table data CAREKARTE outputs and imports.[^7] CAREKARTE is not one of the four systems MHLW has approved as equivalent to the exchange.[^13]

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: CAREKARTE is a facility-first product with a long history (Chōju, from the 1990s) that has bolted on mobile, voice and visit tools. Its most interesting asset is CAREKARTE Link. More than 50 listed partner integrations make it a hub for sensors, nurse calls and visit systems such as SmaCare and ZEST, rather than a closed suite. The two office counts on its own site (19,000 and 21,000) should not be quoted without saying so. For a new entrant, CAREKARTE is more likely a partner, through Link, than a head-on rival in home care. The open question is claims transmission, which its public material does not explain.
{{< /analysis >}}
