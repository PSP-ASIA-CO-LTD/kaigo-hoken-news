*Last updated: 9 Oct 2026*

ZEST ({{< ja "ゼスト" "zesuto" "ZEST" >}}) is a cloud service that builds and manages schedules and routes for home visits. It serves home-visit nursing, home-visit care, home-visit medical care, and residential homes.[^1] It is not a claims system. ZEST imports users from electronic medical records and care-billing software, and sends the schedules it builds back to them.[^1] It is run by ZEST Inc. ({{< ja "株式会社ゼスト" "kabushiki-gaisha zesuto" "ZEST Inc." >}}), based in Shinjuku, Tokyo.[^2]

## Pricing

ZEST charges by usage according to the size of the office, with no initial, base or renewal fee. Prices differ by office, and ZEST gives estimates on request.[^4] ZEST says it is a registered tool under METI's Digital and AI Adoption Subsidy 2026, which can cover up to 50% of the cost.[^6] For how this differs from the care-technology subsidy, see our [subsidy guide](../../guides/subsidies-and-software-buying/).

## Main features

- **Automatic schedules.** Builds visit schedules from conditions set for each service type. ZEST says it reduces long travel and waiting time.[^1] ZEST launched the service in October 2019 and says it optimises across staff skills and time constraints, using patented technology.[^7]
- **Home-visit care tools.** ZEST lists three uses:[^3]
  - suggesting cover staff for sudden absences, taking qualifications, compatibility and travel distance into account
  - optimising routes
  - showing the slots still free for new users
- **Sales support ("ZEST RRM").** Recommends regional partners to visit, drawing on about **300,000** partner records. It also records sales visits on a smartphone.[^3][^1]
- **Management dashboards ("ZEST BOARD").** Visits, occupancy, travel time, cancellations and workload per staff member, across several sites.[^3]
- **AI assistant (July 2026).** On 8 July 2026, ZEST launched the "ZEST AI Assistant" as the first step of what it calls an "AI agent" plan. It has two functions:[^8]
  - answering questions about the system
  - suggesting visit slots for new users, taking travel time into account

  ZEST says the assistant is built on Google Cloud using Gemini and that input data is not used for model training. It is free during a trial period.[^8]
- **Security.** ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification, and compliance with the government guidelines for medical information systems.[^1]

## Links to care software

ZEST's site shows six linked systems as examples:[^1]

- [Kaipoke](../kaipoke/) Home-Visit Nursing and Kaipoke Home-Visit Care
- [Wiseman](../wiseman/)
- [Care-wing](../care-wing/)
- the home-medical EHR "Movacal.net"
- [CAREKARTE](../carekarte/)

Two of these links are described in detail:

- **Kaipoke.** ZEST announced a two-way link with Kaipoke on 7 April 2022, following a business partnership that began in January 2021. ZEST imports users, staff and provision tables from Kaipoke and writes its optimised schedules back to Kaipoke as planned visits.[^7]
- **CAREKARTE.** Care Connect Japan's link page says users no longer need to be registered twice, and that schedules assigned in ZEST flow into CAREKARTE. The link covers home-visit nursing, home-visit care (including disability welfare), 24-hour visiting services and visiting rehabilitation.[^9]

ZEST also announced a link with the shift-planning tool "Synchro Shift" in March 2026.[^10] The sources we opened do not say whether each link uses an API, file exchange or another method.

## Target customers

ZEST's service menus cover four groups:[^1]

- home-visit nursing
- home-visit care
- home-visit medical care
- paid homes, hospices and serviced housing for older people

The case studies on its site include a Japanese Red Cross nursing station, the Shizuoka Nursing Association, Saiseikai Shiga, and private home-visit care companies.[^1]

## Evidence: what ZEST reports

On 28 May 2026, ZEST published results from an MHLW-promoted study measuring the effect of care technology at participating offices. According to ZEST, time spent creating and adjusting schedules fell by up to about **46%**, from 85.1 to 45.5 minutes a day.[^11] These are ZEST's own figures as published in its press release. We did not open the underlying study report.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: ZEST is the clearest sign that rostering and routing, the job the service coordinator still does largely by hand, is a separate product category in Japan. ZEST does not compete with Kaipoke, CAREKARTE or Wiseman. It plugs into them, and CAREKARTE lists it as a link partner. That makes it both a model and a warning for a new entrant. The model is to win the coordinator with scheduling and leave billing to the incumbent. The warning is that ZEST has already built links with Kaipoke, Wiseman and CAREKARTE. Its "No.1" claim rests on a survey of a "designated field" and should not be repeated as a market share.
