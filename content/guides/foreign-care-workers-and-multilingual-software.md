*Last updated: 10 Oct 2026*

Japan admits foreign care workers through four routes. MHLW lists them as the Economic Partnership Agreement (EPA) route, the care residence status ({{< ja "在留資格「介護」" "zairyū shikaku 'kaigo'" "residence status 'care'" >}}), Technical Intern Training ({{< ja "技能実習" "ginō jisshū" "Technical Intern Training" >}}) and Specified Skilled Worker (i) ({{< ja "特定技能1号" "tokutei ginō ichi-gō" "Specified Skilled Worker (i)" >}}).[^1] A fifth system, Employment for Skill Development ({{< ja "育成就労" "ikusei shūrō" "Employment for Skill Development" >}}), replaces Technical Intern Training on 1 April 2027.[^7][^8]

This guide sets out each route, the numbers, the language rules, the limits on what foreign staff may do, the MHLW subsidies that pay for translation tools and multilingual care-record software, and what the vendors we profile say on their own sites about multilingual screens. Most foreign care workers are on the Specified Skilled Worker route.[^2][^3] In this guide, "SSW" means Specified Skilled Worker (i), "TITP" means the Technical Intern Training Program and "ESD" means Employment for Skill Development.

## The four routes at a glance

| Route | Started for care | Purpose stated by MHLW | Length of stay | Family |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EPA care-worker candidate ({{< ja "EPA介護福祉士候補者" "EPA kaigo fukushishi kōhosha" "EPA certified-care-worker candidate" >}}) | Indonesia FY2008, Philippines FY2009, Vietnam FY2014[^13] | Stronger economic partnership between the two countries[^1] | Four years of work and training at the host as a candidate, in principle with no change of employer. After passing the national exam, the person can stay and work as a certified care worker[^12][^1] | Not stated on the pages opened |
| Care residence status ({{< ja "介護" "kaigo" "care" >}}) | 1 Sep 2017[^1] | Admitting foreign nationals in professional and technical fields[^1] | No limit on the number of renewals[^1] | Spouse and children may accompany[^1] |
| Technical Intern Training (care) | 1 Nov 2017[^1] | Transfer of skills to the home country[^1] | Up to five years of training, with a skills test at each stage[^1] | Not stated on the pages opened |
| Specified Skilled Worker (i) (care) | 1 Apr 2019[^1] | Admitting foreign nationals with some expertise and skills to meet labour shortages[^1] | Five years in total. Up to six years if the person meets conditions such as a partial pass in the national exam taken just before the stay ends[^1] | Not stated on the pages opened |

The care residence status is reached in two ways: graduating from a care-worker training school after entering as a student, or passing the national certified care worker exam ({{< ja "介護福祉士" "kaigo fukushishi" "certified care worker" >}}) after at least three years of work as a technical intern or similar.[^1] Since FY2017, training-school graduates also need to pass the national exam. A transitional measure lets graduates up to FY2031 hold the qualification for five years without passing.[^26] From graduates of April 2027 onwards, the qualification lapses unless they pass within those five years.[^26][^27] See [Care-worker training in Japan]({{< relref "care-worker-training-schools" >}}).

Future intake for SSW and ESD is set by a cabinet decision of 23 January 2026, the care-field operating policy for the two systems. It projects a care-worker shortage of about 259,300 in FY2028. After productivity and domestic-hiring measures, it still expects a shortfall of about 160,700, and sets planned intake to match:[^6]

| Item | Figure | Period |
|---|---|---|
| Planned intake of foreign care workers, both systems | 160,700 | FY2024 to FY2028[^6] |
| SSW (i) intake, set as the cap | 126,900 | FY2024 to end of FY2028[^6] |
| ESD intake, set as the cap | 33,800 | FY2027 to end of FY2028[^6] |

## How many, by route

MHLW's March 2026 briefing to the SSW care council gives one figure per route. Each has a different date.[^2]

| Route | People in Japan | As of |
|---|---|---|
| EPA | 3,004, of whom 435 had qualified as certified care workers | 1 Feb 2026[^2] |
| Care residence status | 13,949 | End of June 2025[^2] |
| Technical Intern Training (care) | 20,065 | End of Dec 2024[^2] |
| Specified Skilled Worker (i) (care) | 65,505 (preliminary) | End of Nov 2025[^2] |

The Immigration Services Agency (ISA) has since published a newer SSW figure. At the end of June 2026 there were **79,884** SSW (i) residents in the care field, 18.8 percent of all 425,961 SSW (i) residents, and up 17.7 percent from 67,871 at the end of December 2025. ISA marks these figures as preliminary.[^3]

For the EPA route, JICWELS reports 8,809 arrivals from FY2008 to FY2025 admissions: 3,781 from Indonesia, 3,304 from the Philippines and 1,724 from Vietnam.[^12] For Technical Intern Training, OTIT certified 13,339 training plans in the care occupation in FY2024.[^14] That is a count of plans approved in the year, not of people in Japan. MHLW puts the total of technical interns in Japan in all occupations at about 460,000.[^9]

A free helpline for foreign care workers outside the EPA route and their employers, run by JICWELS, answers in 11 languages including Thai.[^2]

## Japanese-language requirements

| Route | What is required | Source |
|---|---|---|
| EPA | About one year of Japanese training before starting work, to roughly JLPT N3 or above | JICWELS[^12] |
| Care residence status | Graduation from a care-worker training school or a pass in the national exam (the routes above). A separate language test is not set out in the material opened | MHLW[^1] |
| Technical Intern Training | Year 1 (entry): N4 required, N3 desirable. Year 2: N3 required, though under conditions an N4 intern may stay until the end of stage 2 (three years after entry). JITCO lists J.TEST, NAT-TEST, the Care Japanese Language Test ({{< ja "介護日本語能力テスト" "kaigo nihongo nōryoku tesuto" "Care Japanese Language Test" >}}) and JFT-Basic as accepted equivalents | MHLW[^9], JITCO[^10] |
| SSW (i) | MHLW's page: the care skills test and the care Japanese test ({{< ja "介護日本語評価試験" "kaigo nihongo hyōka shiken" "care Japanese evaluation test" >}}), plus JFT-Basic or JLPT N4 or above. The January 2026 cabinet policy: a level judged equal to A2.2 or above on the Japanese Language Education Reference Framework ({{< ja "日本語教育の参照枠" "nihongo kyōiku no sanshōwaku" "Japanese Language Education Reference Framework" >}}), plus the care Japanese test | MHLW[^5], cabinet policy[^6] |
| ESD (from Apr 2027) | Before starting: A2.2 or above. By the end of year 1: the entry-level care ESD skills test, plus A2.2 and a Japanese study plan (no plan needed at B1). By the end: the specialist-level care ESD skills test, plus A2.2 and the care Japanese test | Cabinet policy[^6] |

Technical interns in care must take 240 hours of Japanese after arrival (80 hours for those at about N3) and a 42-hour introduction to care.[^9] The cabinet policy sets the same 240-hour (80 at B1) and 42-hour minimums for ESD.[^6]

Three groups are exempt from the SSW care tests: people who completed stage 2 of care technical intern training in good standing, graduates of care-worker training schools, and EPA candidates who completed their four years.[^5][^1]

## What foreign staff may do

**SSW and ESD work content.** The cabinet policy limits SSW care work to body care ({{< ja "身体介護" "shintai kaigo" "body care" >}}) and similar tasks, meaning help with bathing, eating and toileting matched to the user's condition. Related tasks that Japanese staff in the same job would normally do, such as managing notices and restocking supplies, are allowed alongside. ESD uses the same work definition.[^6]

**Direct employment and a per-office cap.** Both SSW and ESD are limited to direct employment. At each office, the number of SSW (i) workers may not exceed the total number of full-time Japanese and similar care staff. The same cap applies to ESD workers.[^6]

**Home-visit care.** Home-visit services were opened to technical interns from 1 April 2025 and to SSW workers from 21 April 2025.[^11] The worker must have completed the introductory care-worker course ({{< ja "介護職員初任者研修" "kaigo shokuin shoninsha kenshū" "introductory care-worker training" >}}) or similar and, in principle, have at least one year of experience in care offices.[^11] The office must explain the arrangement in advance to users and families. MHLW publishes separate explanation forms for workers with one year or more and with less than one year of experience.[^11] The office must also meet five conditions:[^11]

1. training on the basics of home-visit work;
2. a period of accompanied visits by a supervisor or similar;
3. a careful explanation of the work, and a career-up plan made with the worker;
4. a harassment-consultation desk and other prevention measures;
5. an environment, **including the use of information and communication technology**, that lets the worker respond properly when something unexpected happens at a user's home.

The office files documents with JICWELS, the visiting and inspection body, before starting.[^11] For SSW, each worker needs a certificate of conformity ({{< ja "適合確認書" "tekigō kakuninsho" "certificate of conformity" >}}) from the SSW/ESD care council.[^5] The career-up plan runs for a year. Each new plan is due within four months of the end of the previous plan period.[^11][^5] The cabinet policy carries the same five conditions into ESD.[^6]

**Technical Intern Training: office and night-work conditions.** The office must have been open for three years or more. When interns do night shifts or other work with few staff, the office must take measures to keep users safe. MHLW notes that the industry guideline expects interns to work alongside other care staff and, as a best-effort rule, limits night shifts to interns in their second year or later.[^9] The cabinet policy sets similar three-year and night-work conditions for ESD.[^6]

## The switch to Employment for Skill Development

The law creating ESD was promulgated on 21 June 2024 (Act No. 60 of 2024) and mostly takes effect on 1 April 2027.[^8] ISA says ESD starts, and the SSW system is revised, on that date. Supervising organisations could apply for permits from 15 April 2026, and training plans could be submitted for certification from 1 September 2026.[^7]

Technical interns already in Japan on 1 April 2027 can continue under their certified plans. So can interns whose plans were filed by 31 March 2027 and who start by 30 June 2027.[^7]

For care, the cabinet policy sets a three-year ESD period built around body care. A worker may change employer at their own wish after a two-year restriction period. An employer that sets a restriction longer than one year must meet conditions such as holding the care-worker pay-improvement add-on and must make a career-support plan for each worker.[^6]

## MHLW money for translation tools and multilingual software

Two MHLW programmes, both run through prefectures, name translation tools or multilingual care software as eligible costs:

| Programme | What it pays for | Budget | Subsidy rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retention promotion for foreign care workers ({{< ja "外国人介護人材定着促進事業" "gaikokujin kaigo jinzai teichaku sokushin jigyō" "foreign care worker retention promotion programme" >}}) | Tools that help foreign care workers, named as "portable translation devices, multilingual care-record software, e-learning systems", plus training, study sessions and internal rules so the tools are used[^2] | ¥0.12bn, FY2025 supplementary budget[^2] | National 1/2, prefecture 1/4, office 1/4[^2] |
| Environment improvement for offices accepting foreign care workers ({{< ja "外国人介護人材受入施設等環境整備事業" "gaikokujin kaigo jinzai ukeire shisetsu-tō kankyō seibi jigyō" "environment improvement programme for offices accepting foreign care workers" >}}) | Costs of introducing "multilingual translation devices needed for care work", cross-cultural training, study support toward the national exam, living support and mental-health care[^2] | Part of the ¥8.6bn care-workforce portion of the Regional Medical and Care Comprehensive Fund, FY2026 initial budget request[^2] | 2/3[^2] |

MHLW's stated reason for the retention programme links it directly to home-visit work. Since April 2025 SSW and technical interns may do home visits, and the rules require "environment improvement through the use of ICT" so they can handle emergencies at a user's home. The programme aims to ease communication between foreign and Japanese staff, support Japanese study and reduce the burden of record-keeping.[^2] The environment programme is open to offices with foreign care staff on any residence status.[^2]

MHLW also funds free learning material for foreign care workers. This includes learning texts translated into 13 or more languages, and a web site, "Nihongo o manabō" ({{< ja "にほんごをまなぼう" "nihongo o manabō" "let's learn Japanese" >}}), aimed at N3 and N2 levels with its interface in 14 languages.[^2] For how prefectural subsidies for care software work in general, see [subsidies and how offices buy software]({{< relref "subsidies-and-software-buying" >}}) and [METI or MHLW?]({{< relref "meti-vs-mhlw-software-subsidies" >}}).

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: for a Thai vendor, this topic matters more for the product than for Thai staff. Thai nationals are a small share of foreign care workers in Japan: 472 of about 80,000 SSW care workers in mid-2026, and 164 of 13,339 care training plans in FY2024. A Thai-language screen is a nice extra, not a sales argument. The languages that matter are Indonesian, Burmese, Vietnamese, Nepali and Tagalog/English. Two policy facts make multilingual recording a real buying reason. First, home-visit work by interns and SSW since April 2025 requires an ICT-supported environment, and at least one vendor already markets its multilingual app as meeting that condition. Second, MHLW's retention programme names "multilingual care-record software" as a subsidised item. The bar is moving, though. SmaCare already claims 17 languages, so the feature on its own is not enough. A vendor from Thailand has real experience building for staff whose first language is not the system language, and could aim for more than translated menus: plain-Japanese prompts, input in the worker's own language with a Japanese record saved for the care plan and the inspector, and support for the ESD Japanese-study plan from April 2027. Before relying on the subsidy, check each prefecture's call for applications. The national programme is small (¥0.12bn), and prefectures decide whether and how to run it.
