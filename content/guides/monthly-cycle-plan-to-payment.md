*Last updated: 9 Oct 2026*

A home-care office in Japan is paid for a month of service about two months after that month starts. The steps in between are set by ministerial ordinances and by the prefectural federation that pays the claims ({{< ja "国保連" "kokuho-ren" "prefectural National Health Insurance federation" >}}). This guide follows one user through the cycle for home-based services. It names the deadline at each step and the record each step leaves behind, because those records are what care software has to produce.

The example uses home-visit care ({{< ja "訪問介護" "hōmon kaigo" "home-visit care" >}}). Other home services follow the same pattern under their own articles of the same ordinance.

## The cycle at a glance

"Month M" is the month in which the service is delivered.

| When | Step | Who | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before M | Certification decision, normally within 30 days of the application | Municipality | Long-Term Care Insurance Act, Art. 27[^1] |
| Before M | Assessment at home, draft plan, service-manager meeting, written consent, plan delivered | Care manager | Care-management standards, Art. 13[^2] |
| Before M | Individual service plan written in line with the care plan | Service coordinator at the provider | Home-service standards, Art. 24[^3] |
| Each month | Planned and actual provision tables exchanged | Care manager and provider | MHLW description of the monthly exchange[^4] |
| During M | Service delivered and recorded; at least one face-to-face monitoring meeting | Provider; care manager | Home-service standards, Art. 19; care-management standards, Art. 13[^2][^3] |
| By the 10th of M+1 | Claim filed with the kokuho-ren; care manager's benefit-management report filed | Provider; care manager | Claims order, Art. 3; care-management standards, Art. 14[^5][^2] |
| 15th of M+2 | Kokuho-ren bills the insurer | Kokuho-ren | Miyagi kokuho-ren schedule[^6] |
| 25th of M+2 | Insurer pays the kokuho-ren | Municipality | Miyagi kokuho-ren schedule[^6] |
| End of M+2 | Kokuho-ren pays the provider | Kokuho-ren | Miyagi kokuho-ren schedule[^6] |

## Step 1. Before the first month: certification and the care plan

**Certification.** A person who wants long-term care benefits applies to the municipality. The municipality interviews the person and asks the attending doctor ({{< ja "主治医" "shujii" "attending physician" >}}) for an opinion. The decision must normally be made within **30 days** of the application. The municipality can extend this if it notifies the applicant of the reason.[^1]

**Assessment and plan.** The care-management standards ({{< ja "指定居宅介護支援等の事業の人員及び運営に関する基準" "shitei kyotaku kaigo shien-tō no jigyō no jin'in oyobi un'ei ni kansuru kijun" "standards for home-care support" >}}) then set out what the care manager ({{< ja "介護支援専門員" "kaigo shien senmon'in" "care manager" >}}) must do, in order:[^2]

1. **Assessment** ({{< ja "アセスメント" "asesumento" "assessment" >}}): identify the issues to be solved, by visiting the user's home and interviewing the user and the family.
2. **Draft plan**: write a draft care plan with the goals, the services, their content and the fees.
3. **Service-manager meeting** ({{< ja "サービス担当者会議" "sābisu tantōsha kaigi" "service-manager meeting" >}}): call together the providers named in the draft, with the user and family taking part as a rule. The meeting can be held by video call.
4. **Consent**: explain the draft to the user or family and obtain the user's **written consent**.
5. **Delivery**: give the finished plan to the user and to each provider.

**The provider's own plan.** At a home-visit care office, the service coordinator ({{< ja "サービス提供責任者" "sābisu teikyō sekininsha" "service coordinator" >}}) writes a home-visit care plan ({{< ja "訪問介護計画" "hōmon kaigo keikaku" "home-visit care plan" >}}). It must follow the care plan, be explained to the user, have the user's consent, and be given to the user.[^3]

## Step 2. Each month: the provision table goes out, actuals come back

Every month, care-management offices and service providers exchange part of the care plan: the **planned** use for the month and, after it, the **actual** use.[^4] On paper these are the service use and provision tables ({{< ja "サービス利用票・提供票" "sābisu riyō-hyō, teikyō-hyō" "service use table and provision table" >}}). The Care Plan Data Exchange lets offices send the same tables as data; see our [Care Plan Data Exchange guide]({{< relref "care-plan-data-exchange" >}}).

## Step 3. During the month: service, records and monitoring

**Records of each visit.** Each time it provides a visit, the home-visit care office must record the date and content of the service, and the amount it will receive from the insurer on the user's behalf. It must also keep a record of the specific service it provided, and give that information to the user on request.[^3]

**Monitoring.** The care manager must keep in touch with the user, the family and the providers. Unless there are special circumstances, the care manager must meet the user face to face **at least once a month**, at the user's home. Under conditions set out in the standard, a care manager who visits the home at least once every two months can meet the user by video call in the months in between.[^2]

**The co-payment.** For services paid through the insurer, the provider collects only the user's share of the fee from the user.[^3] That share is **10%**, or **20% or 30%**.[^7] The higher shares apply to users aged 65 or over whose income is at or above levels set by Cabinet order.[^1]

## Step 4. By the 10th of the next month: two filings

Two filings are due after the service month:

- **The provider's claim.** Claims through the payment agency must be filed **by the 10th of the following month** for each month of service.[^5] An electronic claim counts as received when it is recorded on the agency's computer.[^5] Claims are filed at the kokuho-ren of the prefecture where the office is located, even for users insured by another prefecture.[^6]
- **The care manager's report.** Every month, the care-management office must send the municipality, or the kokuho-ren acting for it, a document setting out the services in the care plan that the insurer pays directly.[^2] The Miyagi kokuho-ren calls the form that care-management offices submit the benefit-management form ({{< ja "給付管理票" "kyūfu kanri-hyō" "benefit-management form" >}}).[^8]

The Miyagi kokuho-ren's rules show how hard the deadline is:[^6]

- Electronic claims are accepted from the 1st of the month until **17:15 on the 10th**, including holidays.
- The 10th is the deadline even when it falls on a weekend or public holiday.
- The 10th is the final deadline set by law. Anything that arrives later is treated as the next month's claim.
- Claims must in principle be sent electronically or on magnetic media. Paper is allowed only where that is genuinely difficult.

Offices that do not use a vendor's transmission function can use Kokuho Chūōkai's own claim transmission software ({{< ja "介護伝送ソフト" "kaigo densō sofuto" "care claim transmission software" >}}), now at version 10. Kokuho Chūōkai released updates in June and August 2026 to reflect the fiscal 2026 fee revision. It says claims that meet stated conditions must be made with the updated programme.[^9]

## Step 5. Review, payment, and what happens when things do not match

**The payment calendar.** On the Miyagi kokuho-ren's published schedule, a claim for service month M moves as follows:[^6]

- the kokuho-ren bills the insurer on the **15th of M+2**;
- the insurer pays the kokuho-ren on the **25th of M+2**;
- the kokuho-ren pays the provider **by the end of M+2**.

So a visit made on 1 April is paid at the end of June, and a visit on 30 April is paid on the same day.

**Matching.** The provider's claim and the care manager's benefit-management form are tied together. When a care manager corrects a form, the Miyagi kokuho-ren reviews the provider's already-reviewed claim again against the corrected form. If the corrected form contains no planned service, the provider's claim is cut in full. If it shows fewer planned units than were actually delivered, the difference is cut.[^8]

**Three ways a claim comes back:**

| Term | What it means | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| {{< ja "返戻" "henrei" "return" >}} | The kokuho-ren returns a claim it cannot process, with an error code. The Miyagi kokuho-ren publishes the error-code list and common cases.[^10] | The provider has to fix the cause; the federation lists typical return cases and how to deal with them.[^10] |
| {{< ja "過誤" "kago" "withdrawal of a paid claim" >}} | The provider asks, through the insurer, to withdraw a claim that has already been paid, for example after billing the wrong user or the wrong amount.[^8] | The amount is deducted from the next month's payment after processing. A claim still under review cannot be withdrawn.[^8] |
| {{< ja "再審査" "saishinsa" "re-review" >}} | A care manager corrects the benefit-management form and the provider's claim is reviewed again.[^8] | Units can be cut if the plan does not cover them.[^8] |

**The outer limit.** Under the Long-Term Care Insurance Act, the right to receive insurance benefits lapses **two years** after it could first be exercised.[^1]

## Step 6. Keep the records

The national ordinances require a home-visit care office and a care-management office to keep their service records for **two years** from the date each record is completed.[^2][^3] For home-visit care, the list covers:[^3]

- the home-visit care plan;
- the record of the specific service provided;
- records of any physical restraint;
- notices to the municipality;
- complaints;
- accidents.

Municipalities can set longer periods in their own ordinances. Kawasaki City, for example, requires **five years**. It warns that missing records can mean the service is treated as not provided, and fees already received may have to be repaid.[^11] Kawasaki also counts the period from the first day of the third month after the service month, not from the service date.[^11]

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: the cycle is mostly a matching exercise. The care manager's plan, the provider's visit records, the actuals table, the benefit-management form and the claim all describe the same visits, and each one is checked against the next. Most of the money lost in this cycle, through returns, re-reviews and repayments after inspection, comes from those documents disagreeing. Software that keeps one record of each visit and generates every downstream document from it attacks the real problem. The two-month lag also matters commercially. A small office that is growing pays its staff for two months before the first payment arrives, so anything that prevents a return, and with it another month of delay, is worth more to the office than it first appears.
{{< /analysis >}}
