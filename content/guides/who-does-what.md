*Last updated: 9 Oct 2026*

Japanese long-term care is a chain of people and bodies, each with duties set in law. A care-software product does not serve "a care office". It serves particular roles inside and around that office, and each role has a legal job to do and leaves records behind. This guide lists 13 roles around a user of home-based services. For each one it gives what the rules require and the records or data that requirement creates. The [monthly cycle guide](../monthly-cycle-plan-to-payment/) shows how these roles hand work to each other over a month.

## The user and the family

| Role | What the rules say | Records and data this creates |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The user** ({{< ja "利用者" "riyōsha" "service user" >}}) | Applies to the municipality for certification.[^1] Gives **written consent** to the care plan[^2] and consents to the provider's own service plan.[^3] Pays a share of the fee: 10%, or 20% or 30% for users aged 65 or over above set income levels.[^1][^4] | Certification application; signed care plan and service plan; co-payment invoices and receipts[^1][^2][^3] |
| **2. The family** ({{< ja "家族" "kazoku" "family" >}}) | Is interviewed with the user during the care manager's home assessment.[^2] Takes part, as a rule, in the service-manager meeting.[^2] Can receive the explanation of the draft care plan instead of the user.[^2] | Assessment and meeting records that include the family's wishes[^2] |

## Planning and coordination

| Role | What the rules say | Records and data this creates |
|---|---|---|
| **3. Care manager** ({{< ja "介護支援専門員" "kaigo shien senmon'in" "care manager" >}}) | Must pass a prefectural exam and training, then register with the prefecture.[^1] To sit the exam, a candidate needs **5 years and at least 900 days** of work in a listed qualification (doctor, nurse, certified care worker, social worker and others) or in listed counselling work.[^5] Assesses the user at home, drafts the plan, holds the service-manager meeting, obtains written consent, and meets the user at least monthly.[^2] | Assessment record, care plan, meeting record, monitoring record, liaison record, kept for 2 years[^2]; a monthly report to the kokuho-ren of services paid by the insurer[^2] |
| **4. Service coordinator** ({{< ja "サービス提供責任者" "sābisu teikyō sekininsha" "service coordinator" >}}) | A home-visit care office needs **at least one per 40 users** (three-month average). The coordinator must be a certified care worker or hold another qualification MHLW designates.[^3] Writes the home-visit care plan in line with the care plan, obtains consent, and revises it as needed.[^3] | Home-visit care plan and its revisions[^3] |
| **5. Community comprehensive support centre** ({{< ja "地域包括支援センター" "chiiki hōkatsu shien sentā" "community comprehensive support centre" >}}) | Set up by municipalities and staffed with public-health nurses, social workers and senior care managers.[^6] There are **5,487** centres nationwide.[^6] Its staff, and designated staff at care-management offices, write preventive care plans for users certified as needing support.[^1] It can also make certification applications on a user's behalf.[^1] | Preventive care plans; consultation records[^1][^6] |

## Hands-on care

| Role | What the rules say | Records and data this creates |
|---|---|---|
| **6. Home helper** ({{< ja "訪問介護員" "hōmon kaigo-in" "home helper" >}}) | MHLW's care-worker training rules set the initial care-worker training ({{< ja "介護職員初任者研修" "kaigo shokuin shoninsha kenshū" "initial care-worker training" >}}) at **130 hours** over 10 subjects.[^7] Home-visit care workers had a job-openings-to-applicants ratio of **14.14** in fiscal 2023. Their average age is **54.4**, and **37.6%** are 60 or over.[^8] | Record of each visit: date, content, and the amount billed to the insurer[^3] |
| **7. Certified care worker** ({{< ja "介護福祉士" "kaigo fukushishi" "certified care worker" >}}) | Since April 2015, certified care workers trained in the procedures can perform suctioning and tube feeding under a doctor's instruction, at offices registered for it.[^9] The qualification is one route to becoming a service coordinator.[^3] | Records of suctioning and tube feeding, kept under the registration conditions[^9] |
| **8. Visiting nurse and therapists** ({{< ja "訪問看護" "hōmon kango" "home-visit nursing" >}}) | Before starting, a home-visit nursing office must receive the attending doctor's instruction **in writing**. It must send the doctor a nursing plan and a nursing report.[^3] | Doctor's written instruction; nursing plan; nursing report[^3] |
| **9. Foreign care workers** | Since April 2025, technical intern trainees (from 1 April) and specified skilled workers (from 21 April) may work in home-visit services if they have completed the initial training or equivalent. As a rule they also need at least a year of care experience.[^10] The employer must explain this to users and families in advance and meet five conditions, including training, accompanied visits, a career plan, and an ICT environment for emergencies.[^10] | Career-up plan for each worker, and a report to the monitoring body[^10] |

## Medical, money and oversight

| Role | What the rules say | Records and data this creates |
|---|---|---|
| **10. Attending doctor** ({{< ja "主治医" "shujii" "attending physician" >}}) | The municipality asks the attending doctor for an opinion on the cause and state of the applicant's condition as part of certification.[^1] Gives the written instruction for home-visit nursing.[^3] | Doctor's opinion for certification; nursing instruction[^1][^3] |
| **11. Municipality as insurer** ({{< ja "市町村・保険者" "shichōson, hokensha" "municipality as insurer" >}}) | Investigates applications and decides on certification, normally within 30 days.[^1] Can set stricter local rules by ordinance. Kawasaki, for example, requires records to be kept for 5 years, not 2.[^11] Sets up community comprehensive support centres.[^1] | Certification decisions; local record-keeping rules[^1][^11] |
| **12. Kokuho-ren** ({{< ja "国保連" "kokuho-ren" "prefectural National Health Insurance federation" >}}) | Reviews and pays claims for the municipalities that delegate the work.[^2] Accepts claims until the 10th of the month after service and pays the provider at the end of the month after that.[^12] | Review results, returns and payment notices[^12] |
| **13. MHLW** ({{< ja "厚生労働省" "kōsei rōdō-shō" "Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare" >}}) | Issues the staffing and operating standards above.[^2][^3] Funds the care-technology subsidy, which prefectures run.[^13] Publishes the care-software function survey that subsidy decisions rely on.[^13][^14] | Standards, fee tables, subsidy rules, software survey results[^13][^14] |

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: read down the right-hand column and the software brief writes itself. The care manager and service coordinator produce plans that must stay consistent with each other. The helper produces one record per visit that becomes the claim, so it has to be quick to enter on a phone by a workforce whose average age is over 54. Foreign workers now need the same app in their own language, plus an emergency channel. The family has no legal record of its own, but it is in the assessment and the meetings. A product that gives the family a read-only view is useful, though not required. The kokuho-ren and the municipality are not customers, but their formats and deadlines decide whether the office gets paid. In my view the two roles that decide whether a new product wins are the service coordinator, who carries the rostering and the plan, and the helper, who has to put up with the app every day.
{{< /analysis >}}
