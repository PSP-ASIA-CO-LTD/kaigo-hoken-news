*Last updated: 9 Oct 2026*

A Japanese care office that wants public money towards care software has two national routes in fiscal 2026:

- **METI's route.** The Digital and AI Adoption Subsidy 2026 ({{< ja "デジタル化・AI導入補助金2026" "dejitaru-ka, AI dōnyū hojokin nisen-nijūroku" "Digital and AI Adoption Subsidy 2026" >}}), formerly the IT Introduction Subsidy ({{< ja "IT導入補助金" "IT dōnyū hojokin" "IT Introduction Subsidy" >}}).[^5] It is open to small and medium businesses in every industry.
- **MHLW's route.** The care-technology retention support project ({{< ja "介護テクノロジー定着支援事業" "kaigo tekunorojī teichaku shien jigyō" "care-technology retention support project" >}}), which prefectures run for care offices only.[^1]

This guide compares the two. Our [subsidy guide]({{< relref "subsidies-and-software-buying" >}}) covers MHLW's route in more detail, including the software conditions and the staff-based caps.

## At a glance

| Item | METI: Digital and AI Adoption Subsidy 2026 (normal track) | MHLW: care-technology retention support project |
|---|---|---|
| Who runs it | SME Agency programme; SMRJ funds it, and a secretariat run by TOPPAN handles applications[^6][^4] | Prefectures are the implementing bodies[^1] |
| Who can apply | Small and medium businesses, including medical and social-welfare corporations with up to 300 regular employees[^6] | Every office providing Long-Term Care Insurance Act services, plus two kinds of older people's homes[^1] |
| Budget | Part of the "SME productivity revolution" programme, ¥340.0 billion in the fiscal 2025 supplementary budget[^4] | "Care technology adoption, collaboration and management improvement" programme, ¥22.0 billion in the draft fiscal 2025 supplementary budget, carried over into fiscal 2026[^3][^1] |
| Rate | 1/2; 2/3 for firms with many staff paid close to the minimum wage[^6] | 4/5[^1] |
| Amount | ¥50,000 to under ¥1.5 million (one or more business processes); ¥1.5 million to ¥4.5 million (four or more)[^6] | Lower of 4/5 of spending and a base amount: ¥1.0–2.5 million for software by staff count, up to ¥2.65 million with adoption support[^1] |
| Cloud and subscription fees | Up to two years[^6] | What is paid in the fiscal year; several years paid up front count in full[^2] |
| Hardware | Not in the normal track. The invoice track covers PCs and tablets up to ¥100,000 at 1/2, alongside accounting, ordering or payment software[^5] | PCs and tablets (leases included) and Wi-Fi work used with the care software, within the base amount[^1][^2] |
| How the office applies | Online, jointly with a registered IT support operator[^6] | To its prefecture, on the prefecture's schedule[^1][^14][^15] |
| What the vendor needs | Registration as an IT support operator, and registration of the product as an IT tool[^6][^10] | No vendor registration with the prefecture. The product must be in TAIS as care technology, or judged equivalent by the prefecture, and care software must meet conditions shown in Kokuho Chūōkai's vendor test and MHLW's function survey[^1] |
| Reporting | Effect reports for three years after the project[^6] | Business-improvement effect reports for three years from the following fiscal year[^1] |

## Who runs each, and on what basis

**METI.** The Digital and AI Adoption Subsidy is one of five parts of the SME Agency's "SME productivity revolution" programme ({{< ja "中小企業生産性革命推進事業" "chūshō kigyō seisansei kakumei suishin jigyō" "SME productivity revolution programme" >}}). The fiscal 2025 supplementary budget gave that programme ¥340.0 billion as a whole. The money flows from the state to SMRJ ({{< ja "中小企業基盤整備機構" "chūshō kigyō kiban seibi kikō" "Organization for Small & Medium Enterprises and Regional Innovation" >}}), then to a private body, and then to businesses.[^4] The name changed from the IT Introduction Subsidy with this budget.[^5] The grant rules for the normal track say the subsidy is governed by the Act on Regulation of Execution of Budget Pertaining to Subsidies, its cabinet order, and SMRJ's grant guidelines for the productivity revolution programme.[^7] TOPPAN runs the secretariat.[^6]

**MHLW.** MHLW's notice of 7 April 2026 sets up the fiscal 2026 programme "carried over from fiscal 2025". It makes prefectures the implementing bodies.[^1] The budget summary for the fiscal 2025 supplementary budget puts the care-technology, collaboration and management-improvement programme at ¥22.0 billion. For technology adoption, it sets the split at four fifths public money and one fifth paid by the office. Within the public share, the state pays four fifths and the prefecture one fifth.[^3] The notice names a grant outline for the programme under the long-term care insurance project subsidy ({{< ja "介護保険事業費補助金" "kaigo hoken jigyōhi hojokin" "long-term care insurance project subsidy" >}}).[^1]

## Eligible costs

**METI, normal track.** The subsidy covers IT tools that a registered IT support operator has registered in advance.[^6] These fall into three groups:[^6]

- **Software.** Purchase price, or up to **two years** of subscription or cloud fees.
- **Options.** Function extensions, data-link tools and security. Data-link tools are covered for up to one year.
- **Services.** Consulting, set-up, manuals, training and maintenance. Services are capped at ¥2 million per application, and maintenance at two years.

The software must cover at least one business process, or four for grants of ¥1.5 million or more.[^6] The process list includes a care-industry process ({{< ja "介Ｐ-０６" "kai P-zero-roku" "care process P-06" >}}). Its examples include care-plan management, service plans, care records, facility and vehicle management, and monitoring systems.[^6]

**METI, invoice track.** PCs and tablets are covered only in the invoice track, up to ¥100,000 at a rate of 1/2. That track is for software with accounting, ordering or payment functions.[^5]

**MHLW.** The programme covers three kinds of spending:[^1]

- care technology selected in TAIS;
- the costs of getting care software into use, such as PCs and tablets (leases included), Wi-Fi work and vendor support before and after adoption;
- business-improvement support.

Products must have a published price and be on general sale. Development costs and communication charges are not covered.[^1]

MHLW's fiscal 2026 Q&A says the eligible amount is **what is paid in the fiscal year**. A one-year payment on a multi-year licence counts as one year. A multi-year licence, lease or subscription paid in full in the first year counts in full.[^2] The Q&A also says the fiscal 2025 limit of ¥100,000 per information device has gone; devices fall within the base amount.[^2] It also allows the cost of changing care software to meet listed national standards, such as the care-plan data standard and the LIFE CSV standard.[^2]

## Rates and caps

**METI, normal track.**[^6]

| Grant requested | Business processes | Rate | Wage-rise plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| ¥50,000 to under ¥1.5 million | 1 or more | 1/2 (2/3 for firms with many staff near the minimum wage) | Bonus points; required for some earlier recipients |
| ¥1.5 million to ¥4.5 million | 4 or more | Same | Required |

Care-service providers under the Long-Term Care Insurance Act, and social-welfare businesses, are exempt from the wage-rise plan requirements. All applicants still need a three-year plan to raise labour productivity by at least 3% a year.[^6]

**MHLW.** The rate is **4/5**, up to a base amount. For care software priced by headcount, the base is ¥1.0 million (1–10 staff) to ¥2.5 million (31 or more staff), or ¥1.15–2.65 million with adoption support. Other contracts have a flat base of ¥2.5 million, or ¥2.65 million with support.[^1] Home-service and care-management offices that exchange data with five or more offices through the Care Plan Data Exchange get ¥50,000 more.[^1]

## How offices apply

**METI.** The office and the IT support operator act as a joint unit and apply to the secretariat.[^6] The office:[^6]

1. chooses an IT support operator and a registered IT tool;
2. gets a gBizID Prime business login;
3. is invited by the operator to an online application page;
4. completes the application with the operator and submits it.

After the grant decision, the office signs, installs and pays. It then files a results report and, later, effect reports.[^6] The office must also be registered and operate in Japan, and must declare SECURITY ACTION one star or two stars.[^6]

**MHLW.** The office applies to its own prefecture. The prefecture sets the forms, dates and selection rules.[^1][^14][^15] The office must make a business-improvement plan, normally after consulting the prefecture's productivity support centre, and report its effects for three years.[^1]

## What a vendor must register

**METI: registration is required.** Only IT tools that a registered IT support operator ({{< ja "IT導入支援事業者" "IT dōnyū shien jigyōsha" "IT support operator" >}}) has registered are eligible.[^6] An operator can register alone or as a consortium with a lead firm and members.[^10] Every single operator, consortium lead and consortium member must:[^10]

- be registered as a corporation in Japan, with a corporate number published by the National Tax Agency;
- do business in Japan;
- have its head office in Japan.

A consortium member can also be a sole trader.[^10] A single operator must have a record of providing or selling qualifying software.[^10] The IT-tool registration rules ask for the developer's name as a separate item from the product name.[^9]

On the secretariat's lists dated 7 October 2026:

- **Single operators.** Kanamic Network, ZEST and Logic, the vendor of Care-wing, are registered.[^11]
- **Consortiums.** Rehab for JAPAN leads one. SMS and ND Software are members of consortiums.[^12]
- **Not found.** We did not find Wiseman, Care Connect Japan or Homenet on either list.[^11][^12]

Being a registered operator does not by itself show which products are registered as IT tools.

**MHLW: no vendor registration, but product listings matter.** The notice sets no vendor registration with prefectures. It does set conditions on the product:[^1]

- **TAIS.** The product must be care technology selected in TAIS, or equipment the prefecture judges to be of the same standard.
- **Care software.** It must handle records, information sharing and billing end to end.
- **Home-based and care-management offices.** The vendor test results and MHLW's function survey must confirm the Care Plan Data Exchange conditions.
- **Facility services.** The function survey must confirm LIFE CSV output.

Our [function survey guide]({{< relref "care-software-function-survey" >}}) covers the survey.

TAIS is run by the Association for Technical Aids ({{< ja "テクノエイド協会" "tekunoeido kyōkai" "Association for Technical Aids" >}}):[^13]

- **Who can register.** Only domestic manufacturers or importers.
- **How.** New registrations are made on paper, by post.
- **Fees.** ¥11,000 a year for the company and ¥6,600 a year for each product in the first year.
- **The "care technology" mark.** An external committee reviews products for the mark, about twice a year, mainly on the papers submitted and the company's website. The association says that products without the mark can still be subsidised if a prefecture decides so.

## Fiscal 2026 dates

**METI.** Registration of IT support operators and IT tools, and applications from offices, opened on 30 March 2026.[^8] For the normal track, the sixth round closes at 17:00 on 30 October 2026, with grant decisions planned for 10 December 2026. Projects and results reports must be finished by 17:00 on 31 May 2027 (planned).[^8] A seventh round is listed but not yet dated.[^8] For a grant decided in this round, effect reports run from 2027 to January 2031.[^6]

**MHLW.** MHLW issued the notice on 7 April 2026 and its Q&A on 15 June 2026.[^1][^2] Each prefecture then sets its own dates. For example:

- **Ishikawa.** Applications from 24 August to 14 September 2026.[^15]
- **Hokkaido.** Prior consultation from 13 August to 17 September 2026. Grant applications are due around mid-November 2026. Projects must be finished by 26 February 2027.[^14]
- **Gifu.** Contracts may be signed only after the grant decision, and payment must be finished by 15 February 2027.[^16]
- **Chiba.** Software introduced from 1 April 2026 is eligible, if it is introduced and paid for by the results report.[^17]

## Can an office use both?

Not for the same spending. Each programme's rules exclude it:

- **MHLW's notice** excludes anything already funded by another subsidy, and names METI's IT Introduction Subsidy as an example.[^1]
- **METI's rules** require that the project does not include spending duplicated with other subsidies from the state, SMRJ or other independent administrative agencies.[^6]
- **Gifu's Q&A** adds that items that have received, or are expected to receive, similar public subsidies are excluded.[^16]

The sources we opened do not say whether an office may use each programme for different items in the same year.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: for a care office, MHLW's route usually looks better. It pays 4/5 rather than 1/2 and covers tablets and Wi-Fi. METI's route still matters for offices that miss their prefecture's window, for products that do not meet MHLW's care-software conditions, and for spending a prefecture will not fund. For a foreign vendor, both routes run through Japan. METI requires every IT support operator, including consortium members, to be a Japanese corporation with its head office in Japan. TAIS takes only domestic manufacturers or importers. So a foreign vendor needs either a Japanese subsidiary or a Japanese partner who registers the product and supports the office through the paperwork. On MHLW's side, the gate for home care is not registration but evidence: the V4 vendor test, an entry in MHLW's function survey and, ideally, the TAIS care-technology mark. That takes months, and the prefecture windows we saw were open for only a few weeks. A foreign entrant should plan for one full cycle before subsidy-backed sales start.
{{< /analysis >}}
