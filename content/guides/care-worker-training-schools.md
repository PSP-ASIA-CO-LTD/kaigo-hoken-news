*Last updated: 10 Oct 2026*

Japan trains care workers who are not doctors or nurses through three main steps. MHLW's 2011 review of care-worker training set the basic career path as Initial Training, then certified care worker, then accredited certified care worker. The same review renamed home-helper level 2 as Initial Training, and merged the older Basic Training for Care Workers into Practitioner Training.[^1]

| Step | Japanese | Hours or length | What it leads to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial Training for Care Workers | {{< ja "介護職員初任者研修" "kaigo shokuin shoninsha kenshū" "Initial Training for Care Workers" >}} | 130 hours[^2] | Basic training for care work in homes or facilities[^1] |
| Practitioner Training | {{< ja "実務者研修" "jitsumusha kenshū" "Practitioner Training" >}} | 450 hours[^1] | Required, with work experience, to sit the national exam by the work-experience route[^1] |
| Certified care worker training school | {{< ja "介護福祉士養成施設" "kaigo fukushishi yōsei shisetsu" "certified care worker training school" >}} | Not covered by the sources we opened | Graduation, then registration as a certified care worker ({{< ja "介護福祉士" "kaigo fukushishi" "certified care worker" >}})[^7] |

For the visa routes that bring foreign students to these schools, see [Foreign care workers and multilingual software]({{< relref "foreign-care-workers-and-multilingual-software" >}}). For the Thai equivalents, see [Caregiver training and schools in Thailand]({{< relref "/thailand/caregiver-training-and-schools" >}}).

## Initial Training (130 hours)

MHLW's detailed rules for Initial Training are addressed to the prefectures, which either run the training themselves or designate training providers.[^2] The course has ten subjects and 130 hours in total.[^2] The rules were last revised on 31 March 2026.[^2]

The rules allow four ways of teaching:[^2]

| Mode | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Face-to-face | Default |
| Video conferencing ({{< ja "テレビ電話装置等" "terebi denwa sōchi tō" "video-call equipment and similar" >}}) | Trainees must be able to ask the instructor questions. The provider must check that trainees are watching, for example by keeping each trainee's screen visible, and test understanding with quizzes, surveys or reports. A viewing room or a face-to-face course must be offered to anyone without a connection |
| Recorded video | The same conditions, plus written feedback, interviews or other guidance |
| Correspondence ({{< ja "通信学習" "tsūshin gakushū" "correspondence study" >}}) | Up to 40.5 of the 130 hours in total, within a cap for each subject |

Hands-on practice and placement subjects must be taught face to face, or at a separate venue with an instructor present that has the same effect as face-to-face teaching.[^2]

## Practitioner Training (450 hours)

The 2007 amendment to the Certified Social Workers and Certified Care Workers Act required Practitioner Training for experienced workers who take the national exam. The 2011 review set it at 450 hours, down from the 600 hours assumed in 2007. It also proposed correspondence teaching, local venues, credit for earlier training such as home-helper level 2, and help with fees, so that people could train while working.[^1]

MHLW's list of designated Practitioner Training courses had **1,331 courses** on 1 April 2026.[^3] For each course the list gives a type: correspondence ({{< ja "通信" "tsūshin" "correspondence" >}}), daytime classes or evening classes.[^3] We could read the type for 1,123 of the 1,331 entries with a text tool. Of those, 916 were correspondence courses, 206 daytime and one evening (our count).[^3] The other 208 entries were not readable that way, so these shares are approximate.

## Certified care worker training schools

The association of training schools, {{< ja "日本介護福祉士養成施設協会" "Nihon Kaigo Fukushishi Yōsei Shisetsu Kyōkai" "Japan association of certified care worker training schools" >}} (Kaiyōkyō), surveys its member schools each April. The figures cover member schools that answered, not every school in Japan.[^4]

| FY (April intake) | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Courses | 314 | 296 | 279 | 272 | 266 |
| Places | 12,467 | 12,089 | 11,139 | 11,001 | 10,572 |
| Entrants | 6,802 | 6,197 | 6,546 | 7,356 | 7,780 |
| of whom new school-leavers and similar | 4,296 | 3,930 | 3,084 | 2,933 | 2,756 |
| of whom unemployed people on job training | 626 | 465 | 408 | 349 | 312 |
| of whom foreign students (countries) | 1,880 (22) | 1,802 (25) | 3,054 (20) | 4,074 (23) | 4,712 (25) |
| Places filled | 54.6% | 51.3% | 58.8% | 66.9% | 73.6% |

Source for the table: Kaiyōkyō survey, FY2022 to FY2026. Course counts include schools that have stopped recruiting.[^4]

The number of courses fell by 48 in four years, while entrants rose after 2023 because of foreign students. In FY2026 foreign students were 4,712 of 7,780 entrants, or 60.6% (our calculation). Japanese school-leavers fell from 4,296 to 2,756.[^4]

**Foreign students by country** (largest groups):[^4]

| Country | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nepal | 383 | 367 | 1,311 | 1,899 | 2,539 |
| Myanmar | 165 | 318 | 533 | 747 | 904 |
| Vietnam | 629 | 430 | 431 | 490 | 377 |
| China | 255 | 213 | 223 | 239 | 303 |
| Indonesia | 112 | 207 | 205 | 170 | 172 |
| Sri Lanka | 37 | 29 | 115 | 127 | 134 |
| Philippines | 129 | 85 | 95 | 192 | 64 |
| Thailand | 75 | 57 | 16 | 27 | 16 |

Thai entrants fell from 75 in FY2022 to 16 in FY2026.[^4]

**Who runs the schools.** The sources we opened do not break the schools down by type of operator, such as vocational colleges, junior colleges or universities. The national exam data show a separate route through welfare high schools ({{< ja "福祉系高等学校" "fukushi-kei kōtō gakkō" "welfare-track high school" >}}).[^5]

## The national exam

The national certified care worker exam ({{< ja "介護福祉士国家試験" "kaigo fukushishi kokka shiken" "national certified care worker examination" >}}) is held by the Social Welfare Promotion and Examination Center ({{< ja "社会福祉振興・試験センター" "Shakai Fukushi Shinkō Shiken Sentā" "Social Welfare Promotion and Examination Center" >}}), which MHLW has designated for the purpose.[^5] The 38th exam was held on 25 January 2026 in 35 prefectures. Results were announced on 16 March 2026.[^5]

| Exam | 34th | 35th | 36th | 37th | 38th (Jan 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Examinees | 83,082 | 79,151 | 74,595 | 75,387 | 78,469 |
| Passed | 60,099 | 66,711 | 61,747 | 58,992 | 54,987 |
| Pass rate | 72.3% | 84.3% | 82.8% | 78.3% | 70.1% |

Source for the table: SSSC.[^5]

| 38th exam, by route | Examinees | Passed | Pass rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training school | 7,824 | 4,603 | 58.8% |
| Care staff in social welfare facilities | 48,612 | 33,893 | 69.7% |
| Home-visit care workers | 9,706 | 7,985 | 82.3% |
| Welfare high schools | 2,024 | 1,839 | 90.9% |

Source for the table: SSSC, 38th exam results (selected rows).[^5] Japan had 2,061,643 registered certified care workers at the end of January 2026.[^5] EPA candidates passed at 31.8% (380 people) in the 38th exam.[^6] We did not find an official pass rate for foreign students in the training-school route.

## The transitional measure for school graduates, changed in 2026

Since 1 April 2017, graduates of training schools must also pass the national exam to register.[^7] A transitional measure ({{< ja "経過措置" "keika sochi" "transitional measure" >}}) lets graduates who have not passed hold the qualification for five years from the April after they graduate.[^7] The Act amending the Social Welfare Act and related laws, promulgated on 25 June 2026, changes this:[^7][^8]

| Graduated | Holds the qualification for five years without passing? | After five years |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Apr 2017 to 31 Mar 2027 | Yes | Keeps it by passing the exam **or** by working in care continuously for the five years |
| 1 Apr 2027 to 31 Mar 2032 | Yes | Keeps it **only** by passing the exam |

Source for the table: SSSC and MHLW.[^7][^8] The end of the measure moved from graduates up to 31 March 2027 to graduates up to 31 March 2032.[^7] The semi-certified care worker qualification ({{< ja "准介護福祉士" "jun-kaigo fukushishi" "semi-certified care worker" >}}) is abolished from 1 April 2027. People who already hold it keep it.[^7][^8]

## Software and online teaching

The sources we opened say the following about software and online tools in care-worker training:

- **Initial Training** may be taught by video conference or recorded video, with the checks above, and up to 40.5 hours by correspondence.[^2]
- **Practitioner Training** is mostly offered as correspondence courses on MHLW's list (see above).[^3]
- **MHLW's care-Japanese learning programme** (draft FY2026 guidelines, before the budget passed). The contractor must run web content for foreign care workers. Users must be able to track their own progress and study time, and get study advice based on their learning. The contractor must also analyse learning effects with experts in care, Japanese and e-learning, and report users, study time and use of each item to MHLW. The programme also funds translated study texts for the Specified Skilled Worker care tests, translated question-and-answer and terminology materials for foreign people aiming at the national exam, and video materials with a manual for prefectures running online training.[^9]

We found no source on which software, such as care-record systems, the training schools themselves use in class.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: the schools are shrinking in number (266 member courses, down 48 in four years) and now depend on foreign students, mostly from Nepal and Myanmar. Students from Thailand are very few. Foreign students taking the school route have a hard exam to pass, and from April 2027 graduates must pass within five years or lose the qualification. That makes exam preparation, in plain Japanese with support in the student's own language, the strongest need these schools have. The rules already accept video and recorded teaching for Initial Training, and most Practitioner Training is by correspondence, so online tools are normal. A trial partnership that lets students practise care records in a real care-record app, with multilingual help and the record kept in Japanese, could suit both the schools and the employers who hire their graduates. A first step is to ask Kaiyōkyō and two or three schools with many foreign students what they use now. The sources we opened do not say.
