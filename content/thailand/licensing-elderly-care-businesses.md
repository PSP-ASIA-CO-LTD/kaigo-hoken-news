*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

In Thailand, a private business that looks after older or dependent people is licensed as a "health establishment", the same legal category as spas and massage shops. The law is the Health Establishment Act B.E. 2559 (2016), พระราชบัญญัติสถานประกอบการเพื่อสุขภาพ (Phraratchabanyat Sathan Prakop Kan Phuea Sukkhaphap, Health Establishment Act).[^1] A 2020 ministerial regulation, กฎกระทรวง (kot krasuang, ministerial regulation), added "elderly or dependent-person care" to the businesses that the Act covers.[^2] A second 2020 regulation sets the standards for premises, safety and service.[^3]

The regulator is the Department of Health Service Support (DHSS), กรมสนับสนุนบริการสุขภาพ (Krom Sanapsanun Borikan Sukkhaphap), abbreviated สบส., in the Ministry of Public Health (MOPH).[^1][^5] This guide covers that licensing regime. It does not cover hospitals and clinics, which fall under a separate law on medical facilities.[^2]

## The law in brief

| Item | What the law says | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Act | Health Establishment Act B.E. 2559, Royal Gazette vol. 133, part 30 Kor, 31 March 2016 | [^1] |
| Minister in charge | The Minister of Public Health, who may issue ministerial regulations, set fees within the Act's schedule, and add "other businesses" to the Act | s. 4[^1] |
| Licensing authority | ผู้อนุญาต (phu anuyat, licensing authority): the Director-General of DHSS or a person the Director-General assigns | s. 3[^1] |
| Regulation bringing care homes in | Ministerial Regulation designating elderly or dependent-person care as another business in health establishments B.E. 2563, signed 20 July 2020, Royal Gazette vol. 137, part 61 Kor, 31 July 2020 | [^2] |
| Standards regulation | Ministerial Regulation on standards of premises, safety and service for elderly or dependent-person care establishments B.E. 2563, same Gazette issue | [^3] |
| In force | "When 180 days have passed from the date of publication" (clause 1).[^2] DHSS and the citizen guide give 27 January 2021.[^4][^9] Counted the courts' way the date would be 28 January 2021: see the note below the table | [^2][^4][^9][^10] |

**Why two dates.** Thai lawyers count a period "from the date of publication in the Royal Gazette", ราชกิจจานุเบกษา (Ratchakitchanubeksa), in two ways. The Council of State (Krisdika), สำนักงานคณะกรรมการกฤษฎีกา (Samnakngan Khana Kammakan Kritsadika), counts the publication day itself as day one. The Supreme Court has applied section 193/3 of the Civil and Commercial Code, which leaves out the first day, so counting starts the day after.[^10][^11] With publication on 31 July 2020, the first method ends the 180 days on 26 January 2021, so the regulation applies from 27 January 2021. The second ends them on 27 January, so it applies from 28 January 2021. DHSS uses the first method. Its January 2021 announcement gives 27 January 2021, and gives existing businesses 27 January to 25 July 2021 to apply, which is also 180 days counted from the first day.[^9] This guide uses 27 January 2021, the date the regulator applies. The one-day difference could matter only in a dispute about something done on 27 January 2021.

The regulation defines the business, กิจการการดูแลผู้สูงอายุหรือผู้มีภาวะพึ่งพิง (kitchakan kan dulae phu sung-ayu rue phu mi phawa phuengphing, elderly or dependent-person care business), as a business that cares for, promotes, rehabilitates or gives palliative support to older or dependent people with health problems, through daytime activities, help with daily living, or a place to stay or be cared for. Care given inside a licensed medical facility is excluded.[^2]

## Three kinds of service

The regulation splits the business into three service types.[^2] A licensee must run the type of establishment named on its licence (s. 24).[^1]

| Type | Service | Overnight stay |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daytime care for older or dependent people, with care, health-promotion and rehabilitation activities | No |
| 2 | Care for older people with promotion and rehabilitation activities, with a place to live | Residence provided |
| 3 | Care and palliative support for older or dependent people, with care, promotion and rehabilitation activities | Yes |

Source for the table: the 2020 designation regulation, clause 4.[^2] Types 2 and 3 must have a licensed operator.[^4]

## Three licences: the business, the operator and the caregiver

The Act separates three roles.[^1]

| Role | Thai term | What is needed | Main conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licensee (the business owner) | ผู้รับอนุญาต (phu rap anuyat, licensee) | A business licence from the licensing authority (s. 12) | Aged at least 20, and not barred under s. 13, for example by bankruptcy, certain criminal convictions, or a licence revoked less than two years before. For a company, the person authorised to act for it must also qualify.[^1][^4] Licence valid for five years (s. 14), with an annual fee (s. 15)[^1] |
| Operator (the manager) | ผู้ดำเนินการ (phu damnoen kan, operator) | An operator licence (s. 20) | Aged at least 20, holds a certificate in health services accredited by DHSS, and has passed a DHSS test and assessment (s. 21)[^1] |
| Service provider (the caregiver) | ผู้ให้บริการ (phu hai borikan, service provider) | Registration with the licensing authority (s. 22) | Aged at least 18, and holds a certificate in health services accredited by DHSS (s. 23)[^1] |

The licensee must keep an operator on site during all opening hours, keep a register of operators and caregivers, keep the premises, safety and service to the licensed standard, and employ only registered caregivers (s. 28).[^1] The operator must write work manuals, supervise the service and equipment, ask for and record each client's basic health data and screen clients, and keep caregivers to the manuals (s. 29).[^1] DHSS calls the caregiver registration certificate form "สพส.14".[^5] For which courses lead to the certificates, see [Caregiver training and schools]({{< relref "caregiver-training-and-schools" >}}).

Businesses already running before the regulation took effect had 180 days to apply for their licences or registration, and could keep operating until told they had been refused.[^2] DHSS gave the window as 27 January to 25 July 2021.[^9]

## Standards

The standards regulation has three parts: premises, safety and service.[^3] Selected rules:

| Area | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Premises, all types | A safe location with convenient access; bathroom doors that open outwards or slide and can be opened from outside; level, non-slip bathroom floors; a sitting toilet 40 to 45 cm high with a backrest and a grab rail | ch. 1 cl. 1[^3] |
| Premises, type 2 | A unit that coordinates, raises alarms and arranges services between staff and residents 24 hours a day | cl. 2[^3] |
| Premises, type 3 | At least 90 cm between beds | cl. 3[^3] |
| Safety, all types | First-aid kit and manual ready at all times; warning signs at risky spots | cl. 4[^3] |
| Safety, types 2 and 3 | Additional equipment, such as resuscitation equipment (for example a bag-valve mask or airway device) and a call signal | cl. 5[^3] |
| Service, all types | A client history register in the form the Director-General sets; an assessment of care needs and communication on admission, repeated **every three months**; care matched to the assessment of basic and complex activities of daily living; records of changes in each client's health; a price list on display; a written service contract with the client or representative; confidentiality of client data | cl. 6[^3] |
| Service, overnight stays | Night-duty staff that are suitable, effective and sufficient | cl. 6(11)(d)[^3] |
| Service, type 3 | Coordination with other health professions under the treatment plan, and **no more than five clients per caregiver** | cl. 8[^3] |

In October 2026 DHSS said its checks focus on these three areas. It also stressed that a care home is not a medical facility and must not take in patients whose conditions are too complex for it, or give treatment it has no legal power to give.[^5]

## Inspection, suspension and penalties

Competent officials, พนักงานเจ้าหน้าที่ (phanakngan chaonathi, competent officials), may enter an establishment during opening hours to inspect it, take samples of equipment and products, and seize items that may be linked to an offence (s. 35).[^1] If an operator does not follow the s. 29 duties, the official may order a fix within a set time, and the licensing authority may then suspend the operator's licence until the fix is made (s. 31).[^1] A licensee that does not pay the annual fee on time has its licence suspended until payment, and revoked if the fee is still unpaid six months after suspension (s. 15).[^1] Caregivers who lose their qualifications are struck off the register (s. 33).[^1]

| Offence | Maximum penalty | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Running a health establishment without a licence | Six months in prison, a fine of 50,000 baht, or both | s. 42 (breach of s. 12)[^1] |
| Using "health establishment" or a similar name without a licence | Fine of 40,000 baht | s. 41[^1] |
| Licensee not running the type of establishment named on its licence (s. 24) | Fine of 40,000 baht | s. 44[^1] |
| Working as an operator without an operator licence | Fine of 20,000 baht | s. 43 (breach of s. 20)[^1] |
| Licensee breaching certain s. 28 duties, or operator breaching s. 29 para. 2 | Fine of 30,000 baht | s. 45[^1] |
| Obstructing an official (s. 35 para. 2) | Fine of 10,000 baht | s. 46[^1] |

Where a company commits an offence, its directors or managers responsible may be punished too (s. 47). The licensing authority may settle offences by fine under rules set by the Director-General (s. 48).[^1]

## How to apply

The government's citizen guide for this licence sets out the steps.[^4]

1. Register a user account on the DHSS system at esta.hss.moph.go.th. The guide says applications for this business type must be made **electronically only**.[^4]
2. File the application form สพส.1 online with scanned documents. The guide lists: identity card, power of attorney if used, company certificate, house registration, a medical certificate for the applicant, the list of caregivers and operators, a floor plan of the service area, a location map, and proof of title to the land and buildings.[^4]
3. Officials confirm receipt within two days and check the file within five days. Missing items must be fixed within 30 days, or the file is closed.[^4]
4. Officials visit to assess the premises against the standards. A committee reviews the result. The guide allows 50 days for this step.[^4]
5. If approved, the applicant is told to pay the fee within 60 days, then prints the licence and fee receipt from the system. The guide gives two months as the total time.[^4]

The guide describes this procedure for Bangkok. For other provinces it names DHSS's 12 regional Health Service Support Centres, ศูนย์สนับสนุนบริการสุขภาพ (sun sanapsanun borikan sukkhaphap, Health Service Support Centre), among the contact points.[^4] The guide's fee field says "no fee". Its own steps, however, tell the applicant to pay the fee within 60 days of being told the licence is approved, and then to print the annual-fee receipt.[^4] DHSS's announcement of the 2020 rules says a licence fee does apply. It depends on the type and floor area of the establishment, from 500 to 10,000 baht, and is waived for businesses run by state agencies, foundations or public charities. An operator licence costs 1,000 baht and lasts for life, and registering as a caregiver is free.[^8] The Act caps the business licence fee at 10,000 baht and the annual fee at 1,000 baht a year. The actual rates are set by ministerial regulation (s. 4, s. 15 and the Act's fee schedule).[^1] So the guide's "no fee" entry is either an error or refers only to filing the application. The guide does not say which.

## How many licensed establishments

| Date | Licensed elderly or dependent-care establishments | Source |
|---|---|---|
| List published 2 April 2025 (undated data) | 927: 5 of type 1, 17 of type 2, 905 of type 3. Bangkok 307, Nonthaburi 112, Chiang Mai 60, Chon Buri 50, Pathum Thani 41. 60 provinces listed | Department of Older Persons compilation of DHSS licences[^6] |
| 10 October 2026 | 1,203 nationwide | DHSS Director-General, MOPH press release[^5] |

The two counts come from different sources and may not be counted the same way. The 2025 list's own summary line gives 4 type 1 and 16 type 2 establishments and 888 type 3, against its table total of 5, 17 and 905.[^6] DHSS also runs a public search of licensed establishments on the same system ("ตรวจสอบสถานประกอบการที่ได้รับอนุญาต", check licensed establishments).[^7] We could not open it from our network on 10 October 2026 (the connection was refused), so we have not checked the 1,203 figure against it.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: the Thai rules ask a licensed home to keep a client register in a set form, repeat a needs assessment every three months, record health changes, and keep a staff register, all checked at inspection. Those are the same building blocks as a Japanese care record, though Thailand does not yet ask for electronic claims the way Japan does. With 1,203 licensed homes, mostly small type 3 homes concentrated around Bangkok, the market is small but easy to map. The licence count is also rising: 927 on the April 2025 list against 1,203 in October 2026, though the two counts may not be like for like. An inspection-ready record pack (the three-monthly reassessment, the client register, the staff register with each caregiver's สพส.14 number) is a concrete product idea. It should be checked with a few operators and a regional Health Service Support Centre before any build.

## Not verified

- **Which date a court would use.** We found no court ruling or DHSS case that turns on whether the regulation applied from 27 or 28 January 2021. The page follows DHSS.[^9]
- **The "24 January" in the DHSS announcement.** We read it as a typing error because the headline and the application window both use 27 January; DHSS has not said so.[^9]
