*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

Most public money for community long-term care in Thailand is spent by public bodies: the Department of Health, the National Health Security Office (NHSO), local governments, and sub-district health promoting hospitals (see [Who pays for long-term care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-pays-for-long-term-care" >}})). When any of them buys software, it follows one national procurement law. This guide sets out that law, the systems it runs on, the money rules that sit on top of it for long-term care, and what public bodies have actually bought. It is a description of the rules, not legal advice.

A note on sources. We read the Act, the Ministry of Finance regulation and two ministerial regulations in Royal Gazette copies posted by public bodies, because the Gazette site itself showed a bot check to our connection. Purchase examples come from ACT Ai, an open database built by a non-governmental anti-corruption organisation from the government's e-GP data. ACT Ai warns that its results depend on the quality of agencies' data and should be checked against the government's own site before being relied on.[^1]

## At a glance

| Item | Rule | Source |

| Innovation route | Goods and services on the Thai Innovation List may be bought by the specific method if there is one seller, or by selection if there are several | [^7] |

## The law and its three methods

The Public Procurement and Supplies Administration Act B.E. 2560 (2017), พระราชบัญญัติการจัดซื้อจัดจ้างและการบริหารพัสดุภาครัฐ พ.ศ. ๒๕๖๐ (Phraratchabanyat Kan Chatsue Chatchang lae Kan Borihan Phatsadu Phakrat, Public Procurement and Supplies Administration Act), was published in the Royal Gazette on 24 February 2017 and took effect when 180 days had passed.[^2] Its definition of a state agency names local government, ราชการส่วนท้องถิ่น (ratchakan suan thongthin, local government), alongside central and regional government, state enterprises, public organisations, state universities and others.[^2] So a sub-district administrative organisation buys under the same Act as a ministry.

Section 55 allows three methods:[^2]

| Method | Thai | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| General invitation | วิธีประกาศเชิญชวนทั่วไป (withi prakat choen chuan thuapai) | The agency invites every qualified bidder to submit an offer |
| Selection | วิธีคัดเลือก (withi khatlueak) | The agency invites only qualified bidders, at least three unless fewer than three exist |
| Specific | วิธีเฉพาะเจาะจง (withi chapho chaochong) | The agency invites one qualified bidder to offer or negotiate, including direct purchases of small amounts set by ministerial regulation |

Section 56 says an agency must use the general invitation first. Selection is allowed in listed cases, for example when a general invitation drew no acceptable offer, or when the item is specialised and only a few firms can supply it. The specific method is allowed in other listed cases, including purchases up to an amount set by ministerial regulation, a sole qualified supplier or sole authorised agent in Thailand with no substitute, and follow-on purchases needed to complete or continue something already bought, provided the follow-on is worth no more than the original.[^2] Both lists end with "other cases set by ministerial regulation", which is the hook for the Innovation List below.[^2]

## Methods and thresholds in baht

The Ministry of Finance regulation divides the general invitation into three procedures, and a 2017 ministerial regulation sets the small-purchase limits.[^4][^3]

| Purchase | Method | Rule | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to 500,000 baht, generally available goods, services or works | Specific method | Required at this amount | [^3] (cl. 1) |
| Consultancy up to 500,000 baht; or up to 5,000,000 baht where few consultants exist | Specific method | | [^3] (cl. 2) |
| Up to 100,000 baht | Specific method; no written agreement needed, and one inspector may be appointed instead of a committee | | [^3] (cl. 4, 5) |
| Over 500,000 to 5,000,000 baht, item in the e-catalog | e-market, by one-time price quotation | | [^4] (cl. 30(1)) |
| Over 5,000,000 baht, item in the e-catalog | e-market, by electronic auction (bids may be revised during a 30-minute window) | | [^4] (cl. 30(2), 37) |
| Over 500,000 baht, item **not** in the e-catalog | e-bidding, ประกวดราคาอิเล็กทรอนิกส์ (prakuat rakha iletthronik, electronic bidding) | | [^4] (cl. 31) |
| Over 500,000 to 5,000,000 baht, agency with poor internet access | Price inquiry, วิธีสอบราคา (withi sop rakha), with the reason recorded | | [^4] (cl. 32) |

The e-catalog is the product data that suppliers register in e-GP, with descriptions and pictures in the format the Comptroller General's Department sets. It can include service categories the department defines.[^4] We did not find a software category in the e-catalog, so we do not know whether a care-software subscription would normally go through e-market or e-bidding. For purchases under the specific method, the regulation has the procurement officer negotiate directly with a supplier in that line of business, within the amount the head of the agency approved.[^4] (cl. 79)

The thresholds above are from the 2017 texts. We did not find a later amendment, but we did not search the Gazette for one, so this is in the "Not verified" list.

## The Comptroller General's Department and e-GP

The Comptroller General's Department, กรมบัญชีกลาง (Krom Banchi Klang), in the Ministry of Finance, runs the Electronic Government Procurement system at gprocurement.go.th.[^5] The regulation says that procurement done electronically must be done through the department's e-GP system, in the way the department sets, and that documents printed from e-GP are to be used as the procurement documents.[^4] (cl. 9) The department also writes the operating guidance for agencies and suppliers using e-GP.[^4] (cl. 10)

For a supplier, this means the announcement, the bid and the award of most public purchases over 500,000 baht happen inside e-GP. ACT Ai's data, described below, is built from e-GP records.[^8]

## How local governments and sub-district hospitals buy

**The same law, local heads.** For a local government, the "head of the state agency" who approves purchases is the chief executive of the provincial administrative organisation, the mayor, the chief executive of the sub-district administrative organisation, the Governor of Bangkok or the Mayor of Pattaya.[^4] (cl. 4) Sub-district health promoting hospitals, โรงพยาบาลส่งเสริมสุขภาพตำบล (rongphayaban songsoem sukkhaphap tambon, abbreviated รพ.สต.), appear in ACT Ai as buyers in their own name, mostly for small purchases by the specific method.[^9] Many of these hospitals have moved from the Ministry of Public Health to provincial administrative organisations since 2023 (see [Who does what in Thai community care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-does-what-community-care" >}})).[^10] We did not find a source on how the transfer changes who signs a hospital's purchases.

**What the long-term care money may pay for.** The NHSO's community long-term care money reaches the ground through each area's local health security fund. Three sources set the spending frame:

- The 2019 NHSO board notice says the money for dependent people goes to community elderly quality-of-life centres, service units or service facilities, for services in the benefit package and "according to projects approved by" the local LTC subcommittee.[^11]
- The 2018 board notice on local health security funds, as summarised by Hfocus from the NHSO, allows five kinds of fund activity: public-health services by service units; health promotion and prevention by community groups; services by child, elderly and disability centres; the local government's own fund administration and development, capped at 15% of fund income; and epidemic or disaster response. It raised the cap on equipment in community-group projects to 10,000 baht, and removed the fixed long-term care compensation rates, leaving the amounts to the LTC subcommittee.[^12]
- One municipal fund's 2016 annex shows what the long-term care money pays in practice: the care manager's assessment and plan (up to 800 baht per person a year), the family-doctor team's visits, and caregivers (50 baht per visit of at least an hour). Units must report with receipts within 30 days of finishing and return unspent money to the fund.[^13]

We found no rule that names software, apps or IT services as an allowed or barred use of the long-term care money. A 2021 study found that care managers, who are health staff, struggle because the money is run under local-government spending rules they do not know.[^14]

## The budget year and timing

Thailand's fiscal year runs from 1 October to 30 September. ACT Ai, for example, explains that fiscal year 2025 means October 2024 to September 2025.[^1] For FY2027 the cabinet set this timetable on 11 April 2026:[^6]

| Date | Step |
|---|---|
| 20 April 2026 | The Prime Minister gives budget policy |
| 1 May 2026 | Budget-receiving agencies submit their requests |
| 2 June 2026 | Details to the cabinet; public comment opens |
| 16 June 2026 | Cabinet approves the draft budget bill |
| 23 June 2026 | Bill goes to the House of Representatives |
| July to September 2026 | Parliament considers the bill |
| Late September 2026 | Bill presented for royal assent |
| 1 October 2026 | Budget takes effect |

Source for the table: Royal Thai Government, 11 April 2026.[^6] So an agency that wants money for a system in a given fiscal year has to ask for it about five months before the year starts. Local health security funds work on their own cycle: the 2016 municipal guideline asks for a July survey and proposals by August each year.[^13] Among the patterns ACT Ai flags for attention is specific-method purchasing at the end of the fiscal year.[^1]

## What public bodies have bought: ACT Ai

ACT Ai, run by the Anti-Corruption Organization of Thailand, องค์กรต่อต้านคอร์รัปชัน (ประเทศไทย) (Ongkon Torton Korrapchan (Prathet Thai)), collects public procurement records from e-GP and the government spending site, links them to company registration data from the Department of Business Development, and publishes them for search at procurement.actai.co and as datasets at opendata.actai.co. The open-data site says it holds 26 million public projects. It also says the organisation reserves the right to commercial use of the data.[^8][^1] Its automatic "risk" flags are rule-based and, in ACT Ai's words, do not mean corruption has happened.[^1] We do not report those flags here.

We searched ACT Ai on 11 October 2026 for "Long Term Care", "ระบบดูแลผู้สูงอายุ" (elderly-care system), "โปรแกรมดูแลผู้สูงอายุ" (elderly-care program), "ระบบสารสนเทศผู้สูงอายุ" (elderly information system), "Blue Book Application" and "จ้างพัฒนาแอปพลิเคชันผู้สูงอายุ" (develop an elderly app). The relevant records we found are below.

| Fiscal year (announced) | Buyer | What | Method | Amount (baht) | Contractor | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2021 (24 Dec 2020) | Department of Health | Maintenance of the care-manager, caregiver and care-plan registration program (the 3C program) | Specific | 480,000 budget and contract | King Mongkut's Institute of Technology Ladkrabang (a state university) | [^15] |
| FY2022 (29 Oct 2021) | Department of Health | Maintenance of the Blue Book Application | Specific | 480,000 budget and contract | Lapa Systems (ลาภา ซิสเต็มส์) | [^16] |
| FY2023 (9 Nov 2022) | Department of Health | Maintenance of the 3C program and the Blue Book Application | Specific | 480,000 budget and contract | Lapa Systems | [^17] |
| FY2024 (2 Nov 2023) | Department of Health | Maintenance of the Long Term Care 3C system and the Blue Book Application | Specific; recorded as a lease | 480,000 reference price; contract value not shown | Lapa Systems | [^18] |
| FY2022 (10 Aug 2022) | Songkhla Provincial Administrative Organisation | Install a database and information program for older people's health at its elderly care centre | Specific; recorded as a lease | 80,000 reference price; contract value not shown | An individual (sole trader) | [^19] |
| FY2020 (27 Aug 2020) | Department of Older Persons | Develop an IT system to manage the Older Persons Fund | e-bidding | 10,000,000 budget; 5,580,000 contract | MG Solution (เอ็มจี โซลูชั่น) | [^20] |
| FY2024 (9 Aug 2024) | Department of Older Persons | Consultant for cybersecurity of the Older Persons Fund's information systems | Consultant, selection method | 16,900,000 budget; 16,830,000 contract | CloudSec Asia (คลาวด์เซค เอเซีย) | [^21] |

Points to note:

- **The national community care system is maintained for 480,000 baht a year.** Every Department of Health maintenance contract we found for 3C or the Blue Book app, in FY2021 to FY2024, was for 480,000 baht, within the specific-method limit of up to 500,000 baht.[^15][^16][^17][^18][^3] The contractor changed from a state university in FY2021 to a private firm from FY2022. A Department of Health report says 3C was built by the Department with Maejo University (see [The NHSO long-term care cycle]({{< relref "/thailand/nhso-ltc-cycle" >}}));[^22] maintenance has been bought separately.
- **Local purchases are for materials, not systems.** The "Long Term Care" search returned 7,003 projects. The top results were sub-district health promoting hospitals and local governments buying "Long Term Care materials" by the specific method, in amounts from about 9,000 to 134,000 baht.[^9] Search totals mix in unrelated projects, so they are not a count of long-term care purchases.
- **We found no local government or sub-district hospital buying care-management or care-record software for its long-term care service.** The closest is Songkhla's elderly-centre database, an 80,000-baht job.[^19] A keyword search can miss records, so this is a finding about our searches, not proof that none exist.

## The Thai Innovation List

The Thai Innovation List, บัญชีนวัตกรรมไทย (Banchi Nawattakam Thai, Thai Innovation List), is a register of Thai-developed goods and services published by the Bureau of the Budget. A 2017 ministerial regulation makes items on it "supplies the state wishes to promote or support", which gives them their own purchase route:[^7]

- if only **one** seller or provider offers the item, a state agency buys it by the specific method, directly from that seller
- if there are **two or more**, the agency uses the selection method and must invite all of them to bid

**Who checks applications from 1 October 2026.** On 26 May 2026 the cabinet revised the 2015 resolution that set up the list. From 1 October 2026 the National Innovation Agency, สำนักงานนวัตกรรมแห่งชาติ (Samnakngan Nawattakam haeng Chat, NIA), checks applications, with technical support from the National Science and Technology Development Agency (NSTDA). Products that pass go to the Bureau of the Budget, which checks the price and publishes the list.[^23]

**The 2026 criteria.** Under the NIA's criteria for applications from 1 October 2026:[^23]

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Applicant | A company or partnership registered with the Department of Business Development, with at least 51% of registered capital held by Thai nationals or Thai-registered companies, and a continuous corporate-tax record (new firms may show their latest accounts); or a state agency with legal power to sell |
| Thai research | Significant research or development in Thailand, or research transferred to or done with Thai researchers or companies; the applicant must hold rights to the intellectual property, with a freedom-to-operate check |
| Novelty | New and significantly different, incremental or radical; not a product sold so long that its technology is out of date |
| Testing | Tested for quality, safety and environmental impact by a reliable testing body, to international standards for the product type |
| Readiness | Able to produce, deliver and maintain the product commercially |
| Local content | Uses Thai raw materials, parts or production resources, under NIA rules |
| Term | Up to **5 years**, renewable once for up to 3 more years for high-value, highly innovative products in target industries |
| Removal | For intellectual-property infringement, loss of the 51% Thai shareholding, failure to match the application, inability to supply lawfully, unacceptable problems in use, or other cases the NIA sets |

The Bureau of the Budget's criteria page still describes the older rule of registration for up to 8 years, shortened where the product was first sold to the state more than five years earlier.[^24] The NIA's text applies to applications filed from 1 October 2026.[^23]

**Software.** The NSTDA application form used until now accepted, as evidence of quality testing, "quality-system documents in the software research and development process", and asked for proof of the first commercial sale to a state agency.[^25] That indicates software has been eligible. We did not search the list itself for care or long-term care software.

## Data protection and security for government buyers

**Health data under the PDPA.** The Personal Data Protection Act B.E. 2562 (2019) treats health data and disability as sensitive data. Collecting it is prohibited without the person's explicit consent, unless an exception applies. The exceptions include the provision of health or social care and the management of health or social care systems, and national health security and social welfare where the law requires it, with safeguards.[^26]

**Controller and processor.** A public body that runs a care programme is the data controller. It must provide appropriate security measures that meet the minimum standard set by the Personal Data Protection Committee and review them when needed (s. 37). A software vendor that processes the data for it is a data processor. It may act only on the controller's instructions, must provide appropriate security and report breaches to the controller, and must keep records of processing. The controller must put an agreement in place with the processor to control this (s. 40).[^26]

**Cloud first.** On 28 November 2023 the cabinet approved a proposal to bring digital technology fully into government and asked the Ministry of Digital Economy and Society to drive a "Go Cloud First" policy. On 17 October 2024 the committee driving the policy set eight guidelines for government cloud, the first being to consider public cloud services before hybrid or private cloud.[^27] The government also runs its own cloud, the Government Data Center and Cloud Service (GDCC).[^28][^27] A 2025 paper by the Parliamentary Budget Office recommends that important data, including personal and health data, be kept only on cloud inside Thailand. That is a recommendation in a parliamentary paper, not a rule we found in force.[^27]

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: for a care-software vendor, the Thai public market is shaped by three numbers. The specific-method limit of up to 500,000 baht lets an agency buy small systems by direct negotiation, and every maintenance contract we found for the Department of Health's national community-care systems, from FY2021 to FY2024, was for 480,000 baht, within the specific-method limit of up to 500,000 baht. Above 500,000 baht, almost everything goes to e-bidding in e-GP, which favours a vendor with an e-GP registration and a Thai bidding partner. And the long-term care money that local governments receive is project money for care, paid on approved care plans, with no rule we found that names software at all. The Innovation List is the one route that allows direct purchase above the limit, but from 1 October 2026 it needs 51% Thai ownership, Thai research and up to five years' registration, so it suits a Thai developer, not a foreign product sold as it is. Any vendor holding health data for a public body will be a PDPA data processor under a written agreement.

## Not verified

- **Later amendments to the thresholds.** The 500,000 and 5,000,000 baht limits are from the 2017 regulations. We did not search the Royal Gazette for amendments.
- **A software category in the e-catalog.** We did not find whether software subscriptions are listed in e-GP's e-catalog.
- **The 2018 local fund notice itself.** We rely on Hfocus's summary of the NHSO's statement; the copies of the notice we tried did not open.
- **Spending rules for software from the long-term care money.** No rule found either way.
- **Who signs purchases for transferred sub-district hospitals.** Not found.
- **The FY2024 3C contract value and the Songkhla contract value.** ACT Ai shows the reference price but no contract value.
- **Care software on the Thai Innovation List.** We did not search the list.
- **The PDPC's minimum security standard.** Section 37 refers to it; we did not open the Committee's notification.
- **GDCC services and prices.** The GDCC FAQ and PDPA pages returned a bot check.
