*Last updated: 10 Oct 2026*

Thailand counts older people as those aged 60 or over.[^2] The figures below come from the Department of Older Persons (DOP), which republishes house-registration counts from the Department of Provincial Administration (DOPA), กรมการปกครอง (Krom Kan Pokkhrong), Ministry of Interior.[^1] They count Thai nationals whose names are in a house register, not all residents.[^1]

## Older people, 30 September 2026

| Thai nationals | 64,681,262 |

Source for the table: DOP statistics dashboard, September 2026, data from DOPA as of 30 September 2026.[^1]

### Trend

| Date | Aged 60 or over | Share | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 31 Dec 2022 | 12,698,362 | 19.21% of 66,090,475 | DOP data quoted by the Government Public Relations Department[^2] |
| 30 Sep 2026 | 14,530,607 | 22.46% of 64,681,262 Thai nationals | DOP dashboard[^1] |

The two shares may not use the same denominator. The 2022 figure is given as a share of the "total population", and the 2026 figure as a share of Thai nationals.[^1][^2] On the counts alone, the older population grew by 1,832,245 (14.4%) in under four years (our calculation).

In April 2022 the Minister of Education said Thailand had been an "aged society" since 2005 and was expected to become a "super-aged society", with more than 28% of the population older, in 2031.[^3] That is a projection reported in a ministry press release. We did not find the underlying projection table.

## Measures of the care market

We found no official estimate of the size of the Thai elderly-care market in baht. These are the measures we could source:

| Measure | Figure | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| DHSS-licensed elderly or dependent-care establishments | 1,203 | 10 Oct 2026 | [^4] |
| Nursing homes found by the REIC survey | 1,019 (944 in its bed table) | Apr to Jun 2025 | [^5] |
| Nursing-home beds (REIC) | 17,349 available, 14,370 occupied | Apr to Jun 2025 | [^5] |
| DOP welfare-centre places | 1,620, with 8,679 people waiting | 28 Aug 2026 | [^6] |
| NHSO budget line for community care of dependent people | 5,514.37 million baht | FY2026 framework, cabinet 4 Feb 2025 | [^7] |
| NHSO lump sum per dependent person | 10,442 baht a year | From FY2024 | [^8] |
| Old-age allowance budget | 90,000 million baht | FY2024 | [^2] |

How these measures relate is set out in [Public and private providers]({{< relref "public-and-private-providers" >}}) and [Who pays]({{< relref "who-pays-for-long-term-care" >}}).

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: the demand side is large and well measured. 14.5 million older people, about 1.97 million of them aged 80 or over, and the oldest regions are the North and Bangkok. The paid care supply is small by comparison. About 17,000 private nursing-home beds and 1,620 state places is about one bed or place for every 104 people aged 80 or over (our rough calculation from the figures above). Most care is still given at home, which is where the NHSO money goes. For PSP, the size of the market for software is set less by the number of older people than by the number of paying units: about 1,200 licensed homes, the local governments and service units in the NHSO scheme (2,117 local governments in the caregiver project alone), and the training schools. The market is close to home, growing, and still forming its rules.
{{< /analysis >}}
