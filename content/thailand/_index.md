*Last updated: 10 Oct 2026*

These guides cover elderly long-term care in Thailand. They run parallel to the Japan guides and follow the same rules. Each guide is **updated in place**, with a "Last updated" line at the top and a changelog at the bottom. Every fact has a numbered footnote to a source we opened, and each guide ends with a References list. Opinion appears only in the "Tako-San's take" box.

Thai terms are given in Thai script, then the Royal Thai General System (RTGS) romanisation, then an English meaning, for example กรมสนับสนุนบริการสุขภาพ (Krom Sanapsanun Borikan Sukkhaphap, Department of Health Service Support). Thai dates use the Buddhist Era (B.E.), which is 543 years ahead of the Common Era: B.E. 2569 is 2026. Thai fiscal years (FY) run from 1 October to 30 September.

Suggested reading order:

1. [Licensing elderly-care businesses: the Health Establishment Act and DHSS]({{< relref "licensing-elderly-care-businesses" >}}): the law, the three licences, the standards, inspection, penalties, and how many homes are licensed.
2. [Who pays for elderly long-term care]({{< relref "who-pays-for-long-term-care" >}}): the NHSO community LTC fund, local governments, the old-age allowance, Department of Older Persons centres, and private pay.
3. [Public and private providers]({{< relref "public-and-private-providers" >}}): state welfare centres for older persons, licensed private homes, and what the counts do and do not cover.
4. [Caregiver training and schools]({{< relref "caregiver-training-and-schools" >}}): the 420-hour, 130-hour and 70-hour courses, who may teach them, how many schools exist, and where to find them.
5. [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "data-and-it-reporting" >}}): DHSS's online licensing system, the NHSO LTC programme, the Department of Health's 3C program, and the records a licensed home must keep.
6. [Ageing and market statistics]({{< relref "ageing-and-market-statistics" >}}): how many older people, where, and the size of the nursing-home market.
