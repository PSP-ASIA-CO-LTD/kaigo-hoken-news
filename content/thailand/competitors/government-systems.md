*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

Thailand's community long-term care runs on systems built by the state. For the staff and volunteers who use them, using them is usually part of the job. Any private product sold to a local government, a primary-care unit or a care manager will sit beside them. This page profiles five of them, plus one foreign trial. NSTDA's Nirun, the other major government system, has its own [profile]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/nirun" >}}). For how the money and records flow between these systems, see [The NHSO long-term care cycle]({{< relref "/thailand/nhso-ltc-cycle" >}}).

## Long Term Care 3C (Department of Health)

**What it is.** The Department of Health's online program for the NHSO community scheme. The name stands for its three parts: care manager, caregiver and care plan. It registers care managers and caregivers and holds individual care plans.[^1][^2] The Department built it with Maejo University.[^1]

**How it is used.** Care managers register caregivers and write individual care plans in 3C. The program pulls the records of dependent people from the NHSO's LTC programme.[^2] The care manager prints the plan, for the local LTC subcommittee and for the caregiver.[^1] A fact sheet from a 2021 study describes the care manager's duty as entering care plans and caregivers' work in the Department of Health's database.[^14]

**Data.** The government data catalogue publishes yearly counts of caregivers registered in 3C, by health region and province, from 2021.[^3]

**Links.** The Department of Health and the NHSO agreed in November 2018 to link their data.[^1] The 2021 study still found no link between the Department of Health's care-plan system and the NHSO's system, so care managers did the same paperwork twice.[^14] On 10 October 2026 Hfocus reported the Department's Director-General as saying that the Department will build an online platform, "3C PLUS", linked with the NHSO, in 2027.[^15] That is a news report; we found no Department document on it.

**Pricing and access.** Used by scheme staff. No price is mentioned in the sources we read. No public interface for outside software is published (see [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "/thailand/data-and-it-reporting" >}})).

## NHSO LTC programme (ltcnew.nhso.go.th)

**What it is.** The NHSO's web programme for the community long-term care scheme.[^4]

**How it is used.** The NHSO's 2023 workflow chart and a sub-district manual show these steps:[^5]

- the service unit registers screened dependent people
- the local government imports each project with its approved care plans, giving each person's ADL score, care group and TAI
- it records the LTC subcommittee's approval, draws up the agreement and payment voucher, and records the transfer to the unit running the project
- the NHSO processes the payment and transfers the lump sum to the local government
- ADL is recorded over 12 months of service, and the project reports its results

Since July 2025 the programme also carries the economic-stimulus caregiver project. Local governments upload each caregiver's contract, bank details and training certificate, and record each month's work, and the NHSO pays the caregiver directly.[^6]

**What the site shows.** On 10 October 2026 the site displayed the FY2024 payment rule (10,442 baht per dependent person per year against an approved care plan), a data-entry window of 1 to 15 September 2026, regional summary tables with a CSV export, and manuals for service units, for local governments and for ThaiID log-in.[^4]

**Pricing and access.** Used by service units and local governments, with ThaiID log-in.[^4] No public interface for outside software.

## Thai COC (continuity of care)

**What it is.** A web application on the Thai Care Cloud for sending patient information from hospital to primary care and getting a reply, for all diseases and all ages.[^7] Surin Hospital developed it with the Surin provincial health office, with help from the data-management and statistics centre of Khon Kaen University's Faculty of Public Health.[^7]

**How it works.** Before discharge, hospital staff plan care and enter the patient's details. The system records them and sends an automatic LINE alert to the health staff at the destination. During home care, staff and their networks visit the patient and record each visit in the Thai COC mobile app, which lets supervisors follow the work in real time.[^7]

**Scale.** By 10 October 2018 it was used in 39 provinces by a network of 4,898 units, and had covered 292,155 patients.[^7] It won a 2018 public-service award in the service-innovation category.[^7] We found no newer figure.

**Pricing.** Free to its users, per the 2018 report.[^7]

**Relevance.** Thai COC is a hospital-to-community referral tool, not care-home software, but its home-visit records overlap with what a care manager or caregiver records.

## Blue Book app (Department of Health)

**What it is.** A digital version of the printed elderly health record book, สมุดบันทึกสุขภาพผู้สูงอายุ (samut banthuek sukkhaphap phu sung-ayu, elderly health record book).[^8]

**What it is for.** DOP lists three aims:[^8]

1. self-assessment of health by older people
2. screening of older people in the community by health staff, community volunteers and local-government staff
3. reporting older people's data so that partner agencies can share it

The Google Play listing names the intended users as older people, health workers, local-government officials, care managers, health volunteers, caregivers and local care volunteers. It says the results feed a database of elderly health from the individual to the national level.[^9]

**Form.** App Store, Google Play, the LINE Official Account "@bluebook" and a website, bluebook.anamai.moph.go.th.[^8] The Google Play listing was last updated on 7 November 2025.[^9]

**Pricing.** No price is shown on the store listing.[^9]

## Smart อสม. (DHSS)

**What it is.** An app for village health volunteers, อาสาสมัครสาธารณสุขประจำหมู่บ้าน (asasamak satharanasuk pracham muban, อสม.), published on Google Play by the Department of Health Service Support.[^10]

**What it does.** DHSS's 2022 volunteer manual trains volunteers to use the app to work and report. The first function it describes is the monthly อสม. 1 report, which replaces a paper form. It also covers mosquito-larvae, ATK and other reports.[^11] The 2026 Google Play listing describes it mainly as a knowledge, news and guidance app for volunteers.[^10]

**Scale and reception.** The Google Play listing shows 3.6 stars from about 10,300 reviews, version 2.5.10, last updated 18 February 2026.[^10] Recent reviews on the listing complain that it is slow, crashes and makes reports hard to send.[^10]

**Pricing.** No price is shown on the store listing.[^10]

## NEC Mimamori (past trial)

**What it was.** In June 2022 NEC Corporation (Thailand) ran a month-long proof of concept of its "Mimamori" system at an elderly-care facility in Chiang Mai.[^12] The trial combined:[^12]

- an online care-plan manager that replaced paper records and eased shift handovers
- a family app showing health changes and care plans
- fall-detection sensors using 4D imaging radar, in bedrooms and bathrooms

**Results claimed.** NEC said workloads fell by 36% for the care manager and by 48% for direct caregivers, and paper waste by 70%. It said the sensors halved the time staff took to respond to falls.[^12] These are the vendor's own figures from one site over one month.

**What followed.** In 2023 Chiang Mai municipality and NEC Thailand signed a memorandum of understanding on smart-city solutions. Its first phase was IoT devices in health facilities with alerts for falls and abnormal conditions, the second remote monitoring and appointments, and the third telemedicine.[^13] We found no later public report on either the trial or the memorandum.

**Pricing.** Not published.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: the Thai state has built a tool for almost every step of community care, but not one system. Screening is in Blue Book, volunteers report in Smart อสม., referrals from hospital go through Thai COC, care plans are in 3C, and money moves through the NHSO's programme. Each has its own users and log-in, and the one link that matters most, between 3C and the NHSO, is still promised for 2027. The user reviews of Smart อสม. show that "free and official" does not mean "easy to use". For any outside product, the opening is not to replace these systems, which are part of the job. It is to remove the double entry between them, for the care manager in particular, or to serve the caregivers and private homes that the state tools do not cover. That only works if the agencies allow a connection, and none has published one. The NEC trial is the only foreign attempt we found, and it went quiet after 2023.
{{< /analysis >}}
