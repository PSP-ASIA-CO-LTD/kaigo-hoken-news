## What the matrix shows

- **No product links to the NHSO LTC programme or 3C.** None of the six care or clinic products says it exchanges data with either system, and neither agency publishes an interface (rows in table 4).
- **Only one product sells on DHSS inspection.** Caleo is the only vendor whose pages mention DHSS standards (table 4).[^11][^12]
- **Three pricing logics.** Per resident (CloudNurse), per bed (HexaHealth, Caleo) and per branch and user (APSX, MCS) (table 3).
- **LINE is the default channel.** All six care and clinic products mention LINE: five for family reports, and Nirun for Community for the caregiver app itself (table 2).
- **Billing splits the field.** The clinic suites (APSX, MCS) lead with monthly bills and deposits; Caleo publishes no billing feature (table 2).
- **The state tools are narrow.** Each covers one step (screening, volunteer reporting, referral, care plan, payment) and none is a care-home record system (tables 1 and 2).

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: read across, the Thai market has two halves that do not meet. The private products compete on daily care notes, medication records, LINE family reports and, for the clinic suites, monthly billing. The state systems cover screening, volunteer reports, referral, care plans and payment, each in a separate tool. The cells that are empty for every product are the ones that matter most for public money: no product links to the NHSO programme or 3C, and only one mentions DHSS inspection. Prices are also not comparable as listed. A 30-resident home pays 9,000 baht a month at CloudNurse's published rate, while APSX and MCS charge a yearly fee by branch or user that may need paid add-ons for inpatient care. Anyone comparing products for a real home should turn this matrix into a written checklist and ask each vendor to confirm the cells marked "not published".
