*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

Miracle Clinic System (MCS) is a clinic-management system from Everyday Doctor Co., Ltd., บริษัท เอเวอรี่เดย์ ด๊อกเตอร์ จำกัด (Borisat Everyday Doctor Chamkat).[^1] The company says it has worked in digital health since 2008 and that more than 2,000 clinics use MCS.[^1][^2] Like APSX, MCS is a clinic platform with an elderly-care version. Its elderly-care page lists features but no price.[^3]

## Pricing

MCS publishes six annual packages. The home page shows four in detail and names the other two:[^2]

| Package | Price a year | Users | Storage | Selected features named |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | 9,900 baht | 5 | 20 GB | Medical records, appointments, POS, E-Claim, telemedicine, drug labels |
| Standard+ | 14,900 baht | not shown | not shown | Not shown on the pages we opened |
| Silver | 18,900 baht | 30 | 50 GB | Data shared between branches, central stock, MCS PAY, LINE OA, loyalty points |
| Silver+ | 22,900 baht | not shown | not shown | Not shown on the pages we opened |
| Gold | 25,900 baht | 100 | 100 GB | Before-and-after photos, lab and check-up, sales CRM, API to external systems |
| Platinum | 35,900 baht | 200 | 250 GB | Queue with TV call screen, PACS X-ray, ERP (SAP) |

Source for the table: MCS home page.[^2] The pricing page lists five of these packages, with the same prices, and its title says "6 packages".[^4] It adds these extras:[^4]

- every package has a 30-day free trial with all features, and no credit card
- LINE OA connection costs 3,000 baht a year on Standard and Standard+
- extra users cost 500 baht per user per year
- extra image storage costs 1,500 baht a year per 10 GB
- 5,000 text messages cost 3,000 baht, valid for one year
- telemedicine carries a per-use fee
- organisations and hospitals can ask for a custom package

None of the packages is described as an elderly-care package, and the elderly-care page does not say which package its features need.[^3][^4]

## Main features for elderly care

The elderly-care page groups its features under five headings:[^3]

- **Inpatient (IPD) system with unlimited beds and locations.** Add beds as needed, several branches or buildings in one system, room management and bed moves.
- **Holistic health care.** Real-time vital signs from connected devices, individual nutrition, and daily care records that relatives can follow.
- **Medication and documents.** Automatic medication ordering with dispensing status, quotations, invoices and sales documents, and alerts for low and expiring stock.
- **Finance for long-term care.** Advance deposits, flexible instalments, and links to accounting and online payment.
- **Stock and appointments.** Real-time stock of medicines and supplies, online appointments for relatives and visiting doctors, and summary reports.

The page does not name care plans, ADL assessment, incident records or staff rosters.[^3]

**Platform features.** The wider system offers LINE booking and reminders with a digital member card, outpatient records with ICD-10, a wholesale drug marketplace, online payment, telemedicine with documents for the provincial health office's permission, insurance claims ("E-Claim", including fax claims), and API links to accounting systems.[^2] The company also sells a portable telemedicine health station with a LINE Mini App and a laboratory-data system.[^1]

## Target customers

MCS says it suits every kind of private clinic, wellness centres, elderly-care centres, workplace clinics and private specialist hospitals, with a separate version for each.[^2] The elderly-care page addresses centres that charge monthly fees and long-term packages.[^3]

## Scale

The company says more than 2,000 clinics nationwide use MCS.[^1][^2] It gives no count of elderly-care centres.

## Standards and security

The company lists ISO 9001:2015 (2025–2028) and ISO/IEC 29110-4-1:2018 software-engineering certification by TÜV SÜD (2024–2027). It also lists registration as a digital-platform service with ETDA, the depa "dSure" mark, and a penetration test certified in December 2024.[^1] It says the system runs on AWS with daily automatic backups, and that personal data are encrypted under the PDPA.[^1] We did not check these certificates with the issuing bodies.

## Reporting and integration with the state

- **DHSS (สบส.).** Not mentioned on the pages we opened.
- **NHSO LTC programme and 3C.** Not mentioned.
- **Other public work.** The company says it took part in national digital-health projects, naming Thai Stop Covid Plus and a pregnancy-tracking system for four provinces in the north-east.[^1] It lists awards and grants from the Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation, from the technology-entrepreneur fund (TED Fund) and from depa.[^1]

## Company

Everyday Doctor Co., Ltd. says it was founded in 2008 and that it is "entering its 16th year".[^1] Counting from 2008, that wording dates the page to about 2024. The home page says "16+ years".[^2] A company-data record for Everyday Doctor appeared in our earlier search results but was not opened, so registration details are not used here.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: MCS is the largest clinic-software vendor in this section on its own figures, and it is cheap. Its entry price of 9,900 baht a year is below any per-resident care product for all but the smallest homes. Its elderly-care page reads like a private hospital ward: unlimited beds, device vital signs, medication orders, deposits and instalments. It does not mention care plans, ADL assessment or incident records, which are the records a licensed Thai care home is inspected on. That makes MCS a likely choice for care centres run by clinics or medical groups, which want one system for outpatients and residents, and a weaker fit for stand-alone homes that need care documentation more than billing. For anyone comparing products, the questions to ask are which package the elderly-care features need, and whether care-plan and DHSS-standard records exist at all.
