*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

Nirun, นิรันดร์ (Niran, "everlasting"), is a care-management system for elderly-care centres. It was built by the National Science and Technology Development Agency (NSTDA), สำนักงานพัฒนาวิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยีแห่งชาติ (Samnakngan Phatthana Witthayasat lae Theknoloyi haeng Chat, abbreviated สวทช.), in cooperation with the Department of Older Persons (DOP), กรมกิจการผู้สูงอายุ (Krom Kitchakan Phu Sung-ayu).[^1] NSTDA is a state agency under the Ministry of Higher Education, Science, Research and Innovation.[^2] Nirun is not a private start-up. It is a government-developed product that NSTDA offers to licensees.[^3][^4]

There are two products under the name:

- **Nirun (the centre system).** Used by DOP's 12 state welfare centres.[^5]
- **Nirun for Community.** A field app for the caregivers in DOP's community care project. The project is expanding to every district in 2026.[^2][^6][^7]

## At a glance

| Item | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Developer | NSTDA. The about page names its Assistive Technology and Medical Devices Research Center (A-MED).[^5] A second NSTDA licensing listing names its Digital Health and Medical Platform Innovation group (DHCB).[^4] |
| Partner | DOP, Ministry of Social Development and Human Security (MSDHS)[^1][^2] |
| Product type | Care-centre management: dashboards, health assessments, care plans, daily activities, device links[^1][^3] |

## Pricing

Nirun has no public price list. NSTDA's Technology Licensing Office (TLO), สำนักงานจัดการสิทธิเทคโนโลยี (Samnakngan Chatkan Sitthi Theknoloyi), lists it twice. One listing is "Nirun – elderly-care centre management system" from A-MED.[^3] The other is "Nirun Platform – digital platform for holistic elderly care" from DHCB.[^4] Both give the same terms:[^3][^4]

- the cooperation offered is "seeking licensees", เสาะหาผู้รับอนุญาตใช้สิทธิ (sao ha phu rap anuyat chai sitthi)
- the intellectual property is copyright
- the research status is a pilot-scale prototype tested in real working conditions (TRL 7)

Licence fees are not published. DOP staff log in at a DOP-specific address (dop.nirun.life).[^5] The sources do not say whether DOP pays NSTDA for this use.

## Main features

**The centre system (Nirun):**

- **Dashboard.** Charts of the centre's residents by age group and by assessment group.[^1][^3]
- **Assessments.** Activities of daily living (ADL), dementia and depression assessments, tracked over time.[^1][^8]
- **Care plans.** Goals and activities for each person, covering physical, mental and social health, with progress recorded.[^8]
- **Daily care.** A calendar for activities such as physiotherapy and group sessions, with attendance and results recorded each day ("Nirun Day Care").[^1]
- **Device links.** NSTDA's health check-up kiosk for older people and a meal-planning system for older people.[^5][^8]
- **Multi-disciplinary use.** NSTDA says the platform supports doctors, nurses, social workers, physiotherapists, psychologists and caregivers working together. It also gathers health, food, medicine and activity data in one place.[^4]

**The field app (Nirun for Community):**

- Caregivers record each visit and their daily work. The data is summarised into reports on each caregiver's work and on the health of the older people in the project.[^2][^9]
- From each person's starting data (age, illnesses, ADL score), the system produces a "recommended care plan", แผนการดูแลเบื้องต้น (phaen kan dulae bueangton). It suggests, for example, more physiotherapy or closer attention to diet.[^2]
- A caregiver quoted by NSTDA says the app stores each older person's location, so visits are easier to find. He also says it records whether each person has taken up their welfare rights.[^2]
- The Bangkok Post reports that the app runs through LINE. It says the app includes a checklist of government welfare benefits, with automatic referral for people not yet registered.[^6]
- NSTDA says MSDHS executives can use the data to set national strategy for elderly care.[^2][^9]

## Target customers

- **DOP's state welfare centres.** Nirun's site says the system is in use at all 12 social welfare development centres for older persons, ศูนย์พัฒนาการจัดสวัสดิการสังคมผู้สูงอายุ (sun phatthana kan chat sawatdikan sangkhom phu sung-ayu).[^5] The centres it names are listed below, spelled as in our [Public and private providers]({{< relref "/thailand/public-and-private-providers" >}}) guide:[^5]
  - Ban Bang Khae (Bangkok)
  - Wasanawet (Phra Nakhon Si Ayutthaya)
  - Ban Bang Lamung (Chon Buri)
  - Ban Thammapakorn (Chiang Mai)
  - Ban Buri Ram
  - the centres in Phuket, Pathum Thani, Khon Kaen, Nakhon Phanom, Lampang, Ban Thaksin (Yala) and Songkhla

  The work began as a technology pilot at Ban Bang Khae in 2020. At that time the plan was to reach all 12 centres by 2022, and Ban Bang Khae had 234 residents.[^10] For the centres' capacity and waiting lists, see [Public and private providers]({{< relref "/thailand/public-and-private-providers" >}}).
- **DOP's community caregivers.** These are the "caregivers protecting the rights of older persons", ผู้บริบาลคุ้มครองสิทธิผู้สูงอายุ (phu boriban khumkhrong sitthi phu sung-ayu, abbreviated บสส.). NSTDA's English name for them is "Wellbeing and Life Protector".[^9] They work in DOP's "Community Elderly Care and Rights Protection Project", โครงการบริบาลและคุ้มครองสิทธิผู้สูงอายุในชุมชน (khrongkan boriban lae khumkhrong sitthi phu sung-ayu nai chumchon).[^2]
  - They complete a 240-hour course.[^9]
  - The Bangkok Post reports a monthly stipend of 10,000 baht.[^6]
  - This is a DOP social-welfare programme. It is separate from the NHSO-funded community scheme. In that scheme, care managers register caregivers and write each person's care plan in the Department of Health's 3C program.[^12] See [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "/thailand/data-and-it-reporting" >}}).

## Scale: what has been published

| Date of source | Who | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Mar 2025 | NSTDA press release[^9] | 2024 pilot: 19 areas in 12 provinces, 35 caregivers. 2025: 156 areas in 76 provinces, 311 caregivers trained (second cohort), **34,200** older people cared for. Three-year plan: 4 caregivers in each of 7,772 local governments (2,472 municipalities and 5,300 subdistrict administrative organisations), 31,088 caregivers serving 3,108,800 older people. |
| 23 Jul 2025 | NSTDA press release[^2] | 2024 pilot: more than 24,340 older people. 2025: 307 caregivers, 342 in total for 2024–2025, and "more than **342,000**" older people. 2026 plan: 256 areas in 76 provinces, with 536 more caregivers. |
| 29 Jul 2025 | Bangkok Post[^6] | Used in 12 provinces, serving over 24,000 older people through 342 trained caregivers. Aims to expand nationwide "this year", benefiting more than 340,000 older people. |
| 26 Mar 2026 | Thairath[^7] | 2026: expanded to every district, 268 areas in 76 provinces. A third cohort of 587 caregivers graduated. NSTDA's Nirun for Community supports their work. |
| 8 Apr 2026 | NSTDA press release[^11] | DOP gave NSTDA its "outstanding special" network-organisation award for 2026 for Nirun for Community. NSTDA says it will keep developing and expanding Nirun. |

**The numbers do not agree.** NSTDA's March 2025 release puts the second cohort at 311 and the older people cared for in 2025 at 34,200.[^9] Its July 2025 release puts the cohort at 307 and the total at "more than 342,000".[^2] The second figure is ten times the first and matches the caregiver total (342), so it may be a typing error. We could not check which is right. Treat the number of older people as unverified. The caregiver counts (35, then about 311, then 587 more in 2026) and the area counts (19, then 156, then 268) are consistent across sources.[^2][^9][^7]

## Reporting and integration: NHSO, DHSS and 3C

- **NHSO LTC programme and the Department of Health's 3C program.** No source we opened says Nirun or Nirun for Community exchanges data with either system. Their caregivers belong to a different programme (DOP, not NHSO).
- **DHSS (สบส.).** No link is mentioned. DHSS licenses private care homes under the Health Establishment Act. DOP's welfare centres are state bodies.
- **DOP.** Nirun has a DOP-specific instance.[^5] NSTDA says the field data feeds MSDHS policy-making.[^2]
- **Devices.** The health check-up kiosk and meal-planning system are both NSTDA's own.[^5][^8]

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: Nirun is the most important name on this list for anyone selling software to Thai public-sector care. It is already the system of record in all 12 of DOP's welfare centres. Its community app is being rolled out to every district through a ministry programme. It comes from a state agency, so for a government buyer it can look free, safe and already approved. A private vendor selling to the same public bodies should expect to be compared with Nirun on price and on "why not use the government system". The realistic options are to fit beside it or to offer what it does not. The pages we opened say nothing about medication records, billing, staff rosters, family reporting, or links to the NHSO and 3C systems. NSTDA is also openly looking for licensees, which means a private company could end up selling Nirun itself. The conflicting coverage numbers (34,200 or 342,000 older people) are a reminder to quote the caregiver and area counts, not the headline reach.
{{< /analysis >}}
