*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

This section profiles the care software that Thai elderly-care providers meet first. It runs parallel to the [Japanese competitor profiles]({{< relref "/competitors" >}}). Thailand's market is younger and much smaller, and it splits differently, so the groups below are not the same as Japan's.

Each profile covers five things:

- pricing
- main features
- target customers
- scale claims
- reporting and integration with the three state bodies that matter: the Department of Health Service Support (DHSS), กรมสนับสนุนบริการสุขภาพ (Krom Sanapsanun Borikan Sukkhaphap, สบส.); the National Health Security Office (NHSO), สำนักงานหลักประกันสุขภาพแห่งชาติ (Samnakngan Lak Prakan Sukkhaphap haeng Chat, สปสช.); and the Department of Health's Long Term Care 3C program

For what each of those bodies requires, see [Licensing elderly-care businesses]({{< relref "/thailand/licensing-elderly-care-businesses" >}}) and [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "/thailand/data-and-it-reporting" >}}).

Profiles are updated in place and carry their own changelog:

1. [Nirun and Nirun for Community]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/nirun" >}}) (NSTDA, with the Department of Older Persons)
2. [CloudNurse]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/cloudnurse" >}}) (TH Healthtech Co., Ltd.)
3. [HexaHealth]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/hexahealth" >}}) (elderly-care module)
4. [Caleo]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/caleo" >}})

**How to read the numbers.** We found no market-share figures for Thai care software, official or otherwise. Customer counts are the vendors' own, undated and unaudited. Paid "market reports" on Thai long-term-care software publish only a table of contents, so we do not use them. The only founder estimate of market size we found is labelled as such in the CloudNurse profile.

## The landscape in four groups

**1. Free government systems.** The state is the largest software supplier in Thai elderly care.

- **Nirun.** Built by the National Science and Technology Development Agency (NSTDA), สำนักงานพัฒนาวิทยาศาสตร์และเทคโนโลยีแห่งชาติ (Samnakngan Phatthana Witthayasat lae Theknoloyi haeng Chat, สวทช.). It is used in all 12 welfare centres run by the Department of Older Persons (DOP), กรมกิจการผู้สูงอายุ (Krom Kitchakan Phu Sung-ayu).[^1] Its community app, Nirun for Community, supports DOP's community caregivers in 268 areas across all 76 provinces in 2026.[^2]
- **NHSO's LTC web programme and the Department of Health's 3C program.** In the NHSO-funded community scheme, care managers use the 3C program to register caregivers and write individual care plans. The program pulls the records of dependent older people from the NHSO's LTC programme.[^16] Both systems are covered in [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "/thailand/data-and-it-reporting" >}}).

**2. Private nursing-home software.** Small Thai start-ups selling to fee-paying private homes:

- [CloudNurse]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/cloudnurse" >}}), launched in November 2024, publishes a price of 300 baht per resident per month.[^3]
- [Caleo]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/caleo" >}}) launched in 2026 and sells on DHSS inspection readiness.[^4]

**3. Clinic suites with an elderly-care module.** Vendors of clinic software adding long-term residents to an existing platform:

- [HexaHealth]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/hexahealth" >}}) publishes bed-based tiers, but they contradict each other.[^5]
- **APSX** (APS Thailand) has a care-centre page covering the following:[^6]
  - resident registry and care plans
  - daily nursing notes
  - timed medication with interaction alerts
  - rooms and beds
  - staff shifts linked to payroll
  - LINE reports to families
  - monthly resident accounts that close into an invoice automatically

  It says residents are unlimited and packages are sized by branches and staff users. The page's structured data lists three offers from 27,000 to 135,000 baht.[^6] The page does not say what period these prices cover.
- **MCS (Miracle Clinic System)**, from Everyday Doctor Co., Ltd., บริษัท เอเวอรี่เดย์ ด๊อกเตอร์ จำกัด, offers an elderly-care-centre system. It lists the following:[^7]
  - unlimited inpatient beds and locations
  - real-time vital signs from connected devices
  - individual nutrition
  - medication ordering
  - quotations and invoices
  - deposits and instalment payments for long-term packages
  - appointments for families and visiting doctors

  No price is published on that page.[^7]

**4. Foreign entrants.** NEC Thailand trialled its Japanese "Mimamori" system at a Chiang Mai nursing home in 2022. The system combines an online care-plan manager, family alerts and fall sensors. In 2023 NEC signed a smart-city memorandum with Chiang Mai municipality covering elderly care.[^8] We found no later public update. NEC is not profiled here.

## Why Nirun for Community matters

Nirun for Community is the government's own field app for DOP's "caregivers protecting the rights of older persons", ผู้บริบาลคุ้มครองสิทธิผู้สูงอายุ (phu boriban khumkhrong sitthi phu sung-ayu). It has grown in three steps:

- **2024.** A pilot in 19 areas of 12 provinces with 35 caregivers.[^13]
- **2025.** 156 areas in all 76 provinces.[^13]
- **2026.** Every district, 268 areas in 76 provinces, with 587 more caregivers trained.[^2]

MSDHS's three-year plan is four caregivers in each of 7,772 local governments, which is 31,088 caregivers.[^13] NSTDA says the data caregivers enter feeds the ministry's national policy-making.[^14] DOP gave NSTDA a special award for the system in April 2026.[^15] Its sister product, Nirun, is already the records system in DOP's 12 residential welfare centres.[^1]

The published counts of older people covered do not agree. NSTDA gives 34,200 in one release and "more than 342,000" in another for 2025. See the [Nirun profile]({{< relref "/thailand/competitors/nirun" >}}).[^13][^14]

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: in Thai public-sector elderly care, the main competitor is the state, not a company. DOP's centres and its community caregivers run on NSTDA's Nirun, and the NHSO community scheme runs on NHSO and Department of Health systems. Any outside product sold to a public body will be asked why it is needed alongside these free tools. It will also be asked whether it can feed them, and no vendor yet claims it can. In private homes, the market is a handful of very young vendors, each with a few dozen customers at most on their own figures. They compete on paperwork savings, LINE family reports and, increasingly, DHSS inspection readiness. Published prices now set a public benchmark: 300 baht per resident per month at CloudNurse, and a free tier for homes of up to five beds at HexaHealth. The two gaps no one has filled yet are a checkable mapping to the DHSS standards and any link to the NHSO and 3C systems. Unlike Japan, there are no share figures, no public lists of approved software, and no acquirer disclosures, so this map should be refreshed every few months.
{{< /analysis >}}

## Not covered yet

- **Clinic and hospital information systems** used by public hospitals and primary-care units for home visits. These include Thai COC, the Department of Health's Blue Book app and the Smart อสม app for village health volunteers. They are adjacent tools rather than care-home software. We have not yet researched them in depth.
- **Consumer caregiver-matching apps.** These help families hire home caregivers. They are not software for care providers.
