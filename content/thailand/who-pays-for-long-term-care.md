*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

Thailand has no long-term care insurance of the Japanese kind. The public money for elderly care comes through several separate channels, each run by a different agency:

| Channel | Run by | What it pays for | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Community long-term care (LTC) for dependent people | National Health Security Office (NHSO), สำนักงานหลักประกันสุขภาพแห่งชาติ (Samnakngan Lak Prakan Sukkhaphap haeng Chat), with local governments | Care plans, caregiver visits and equipment for people assessed as dependent | [^1][^2] |
| Old-age allowance | Paid by local administrative organisations (LAOs), องค์กรปกครองส่วนท้องถิ่น (ongkon pokkhrong suan thongthin, local administrative organisation), under a Ministry of Interior regulation | A monthly cash allowance by age band | [^4] |
| State welfare centres | Department of Older Persons (DOP), กรมกิจการผู้สูงอายุ (Krom Kitchakan Phu Sung-ayu), in the Ministry of Social Development and Human Security (MSDHS) | Places in its 12 centres for older people (11 of them in DOP's August 2026 residents table; see [Public and private providers]({{< relref "public-and-private-providers" >}})) | [^5][^6] |
| Private care homes | Residents and families | Fees set by each licensed home | [^7] |

## The NHSO community long-term care budget

The NHSO runs the community LTC scheme inside the Universal Coverage Scheme fund (the "gold card" or "30 baht" scheme). It started under a 2016 government policy and works through local governments.[^1] The scheme's full name is ระบบการดูแลระยะยาวด้านสาธารณสุขสำหรับผู้สูงอายุที่มีภาวะพึ่งพิงในพื้นที่ (rabop kan dulae raya yao dan satharanasuk samrap phu sung-ayu thi mi phawa phuengphing nai phuenthi, area-based long-term public-health care system for dependent older people).[^1]

**Who qualifies.** A person qualifies with a Barthel Activities of Daily Living (ADL) score of 11 or less, on ten items such as eating, toilet use, moving from bed to chair, bathing and continence. Scores of 0 to 4 are grouped as bed-bound and 5 to 11 as home-bound.[^1] In 2024 the NHSO board added two groups: people with dementia from the moderate stage, and people at the end of life.[^1] The FY2024 fund notice describes the money as covering community care for dependent people "who are Thai citizens", linking health services with social services.[^2] The same NHSO site, however, tells users that the system now removes ineligible people, naming those without Universal Coverage rights ("non UC") and people who have died.[^2] So in practice the scheme appears to cover Universal Coverage members only. We did not find a rule that says so directly.

**How the money flows.** Under clause 74 of the FY2024 fund notice, NHSO pays the service unit or the local government that runs the local health security fund, according to the number of dependent people with an **approved individual care plan**. The payment is a lump sum per person per year, under rules NHSO sets.[^2]

| Item | Figure | Date or period | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lump sum per dependent person | 10,442 baht a year, up from 6,000 baht | Approved by the NHSO board, announced June 2024 | [^1][^2] |
| People expected to be covered after the increase | About 600,000, up from about 320,000 a year | NHSO board statement, June 2024 | [^1] |
| Budget line "public-health services for dependent people in the community" | 5,514,367,800 baht, up 2,614,121,800 baht (90.13%) on FY2025 | FY2026 framework approved by the cabinet on 4 February 2025 | [^3] |

The FY2026 figure is the cabinet-approved framework, not the final enacted budget. We did not find the final FY2026 allocation.

## Local governments and the caregivers they hire

Local governments sit at the centre of the community scheme. The NHSO describes them as the hub that links health care with social care in their area.[^1] In addition to the per-person lump sum, the NHSO's LTC programme site tracks caregivers (CGs) whom local governments hire under an economic-stimulus project that began in 2025. The figures below are from the site's summary of 10 October 2026.[^8]

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Caregivers employed, phases 1 (Sep to Oct 2025) and 2 (from Nov 2025) | 12,125 |
| Of whom paid 5,000 baht a month | 11,192 |
| Of whom paid 6,000 baht a month | 933 |
| Pay under the agreements | 738,696,000 baht |
| Local governments with agreements | 2,117 |
| Dependent people cared for by these caregivers | 106,377 |
| Caregivers whom local governments want to keep paying (continuing) | 11,273, at 1,877 local governments |

Source for the table: NHSO LTC programme site.[^8] The Bangkok region (NHSO region 13) shows no entries.[^8]

These stimulus-funded caregivers are a subset of the community workforce. On 10 October 2026, Hfocus reported the Director-General of the Department of Health as saying that 135,591 caregivers care for 560,461 dependent people in the community.[^9] That is a news report of a spoken figure. We did not find the underlying table.

## The old-age allowance

The old-age allowance, เบี้ยยังชีพผู้สูงอายุ (bia yang chip phu sung-ayu, old-age living allowance), is a monthly cash payment, not a care benefit. Local governments pay it under a 2023 Ministry of Interior regulation, in force from 12 August 2023.[^4]

| Age | Monthly allowance |
|---|---|
| 60 to 69 | 600 baht |
| 70 to 79 | 700 baht |
| 80 to 89 | 800 baht |
| 90 and over | 1,000 baht |

Source for the table: Government Public Relations Department, Ang Thong office, August 2023.[^4]

Three ministries are involved: MSDHS (through DOP and the National Commission on the Elderly), the Ministry of Interior (through local governments, which pay), and the Ministry of Finance.[^4] The 2023 regulation changed one eligibility rule to "a person with no income or not enough income to live on, as set by the National Commission on the Elderly". People already registered before the regulation keep their allowance.[^4] The same release said the allowance budget had grown from 50,000 million baht a year to 80,000 million, and to 90,000 million baht in FY2024.[^4]

## Department of Older Persons welfare centres

DOP runs social welfare development centres for older persons, ศูนย์พัฒนาการจัดสวัสดิการสังคมผู้สูงอายุ (sun phatthana kan chat sawatdikan sangkhom phu sung-ayu, social welfare development centre for older persons). Its website lists 12 centres, including Ban Bang Khae in Bangkok.[^5] DOP's statistics for 28 August 2026 split residents into a welfare group, กลุ่มสงเคราะห์ (klum songkhro, welfare group), and a self-reliant group, กลุ่มพึ่งพาตนเอง (klum phuengpha ton-eng, self-reliant group). They show long waiting lists in both.[^6] The DOP page does not say what each group pays. Details are in [Public and private providers]({{< relref "public-and-private-providers" >}}).

## Private pay

Private care homes licensed by DHSS charge their own fees. The standards regulation requires each home to display its list of services and prices and to sign a written service contract with the resident or a representative.[^7] We found no official national data on prices. We did not find any NHSO payment to private care homes for residents. The LTC lump sum is paid to service units and local governments on approved care plans.[^2]

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: the public money with a software angle is the NHSO community LTC budget. It is paid per approved individual care plan, through local governments and their service units (2,117 of them in the caregiver stimulus project alone), and its FY2026 framework almost doubled. Every payment depends on an ADL assessment and an approved care plan, so the workflow is assessment, care plan, approval, visits and reporting, close to the Japanese care-manager cycle. The buyers would be local governments and service units, not care homes, and NHSO already runs its own programme site, so any product would need to work with that system, not replace it. Private nursing homes are a separate, fee-paying market under DHSS rules. The old-age allowance is cash and has no software angle.
