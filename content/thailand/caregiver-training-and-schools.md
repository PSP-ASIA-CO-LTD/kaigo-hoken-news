*Last updated: 10 Oct 2026*

Thailand has two separate caregiver training tracks. One is for staff of licensed private care homes, regulated by the Department of Health Service Support (DHSS). The other is a Department of Health course for caregivers, ผู้ดูแลผู้สูงอายุ (phu dulae phu sung-ayu, caregiver), the role the community long-term care scheme relies on.[^6][^11] Private schools that teach the first track are supervised by the Ministry of Education. This guide sets out each course, who may run it, how many schools there are, and where to find them.

## Why training is required

Under the Health Establishment Act, a caregiver in a licensed care home must be registered with DHSS. To register, the person must be at least 18 and hold a certificate in health services **accredited by DHSS** (s. 23).[^1] An operator, the manager every type 2 and type 3 home must have, must be at least 20, hold a DHSS-accredited certificate, and pass a DHSS test (s. 21).[^1] A licensed home may employ only registered caregivers (s. 28(5)).[^1] So the DHSS accreditation of a school's course decides whether its graduates can work in licensed homes.

## How a school gets its course accredited

DHSS says an institution must have its course accredited by DHSS **before** it starts teaching. Graduates must finish an accredited course to apply for registration.[^2] After accreditation, the institution must tell DHSS its teaching plan or timetable before each course opens. After each cohort, it must send DHSS the list of graduates and enter the data in the DHSS system. DHSS uses this data when it registers caregivers.[^2]

For private schools, the Office of the Private Education Commission (OPEC), สำนักงานคณะกรรมการส่งเสริมการศึกษาเอกชน (Samnakngan Khana Kammakan Songsoem Kan Sueksa Ekkachon), sets the order of steps. A school that wants to use a DHSS course first applies to its supervising office (the provincial education office or the provincial private-education office). It then applies for course accreditation at the DHSS Health Service Support Centre that covers its province.[^3]

DHSS said in 2023 that it had two standard course structures for elderly or dependent-person care, and 13 for massage and spa.[^2] It did not name the two in that release. The 420-hour caregiver course and the 130-hour operator course are the two that OPEC lists under DHSS.[^3]

### Inside the 420-hour standard

The 2018 standard published by DOP breaks down as follows.[^4]

| Module | Hours |
|---|---|
| General knowledge of older people | 15 |
| Health promotion for older people | 50 |
| Care of older people | 75 |
| Crisis assessment, life support, first aid and referral | 21 |
| Common diseases, syndromes and problems in older people | 10 |
| Concepts about caregivers | 11 |
| Language and computer skills | 18 |
| Practical training: 110 hours in an elderly-care facility and 110 hours in the community | 220 |
| **Total** | **420** |

Source for the table: DOP, 420-hour standard.[^4] Trainees must score at least 80% in both the theory and the practical assessments.[^4] The language and computer module includes basic digital skills, which the standard gives as "computer, applications" for work.[^4] Assistant practical instructors must have completed the 420-hour course and have at least two years of continuous elderly-care experience.[^4]

OPEC's page calls the 420-hour course a DHSS course and links a DHSS manual.[^3] We did not confirm that the DHSS manual and the DOP standard have the same content.

### The 130-hour operator course: an example

The Chulabhorn Royal Academy, ราชวิทยาลัยจุฬาภรณ์ (Ratchawitthayalai Chulaphon), opened applications for its 130-hour operator certificate for the 2026 academic year, closing 7 August 2026, with 30 places. Applicants must be at least 20 and hold at least a bachelor's degree.[^5]

## How many schools

| Count | Figure | Date | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Institutions with DHSS-accredited elderly-care courses | 159 (plus 375 for massage and spa, 534 in total) | 10 August 2023 | DHSS[^2] |
| Non-formal private vocational schools in the "care school" group, โรงเรียนบริบาล (rongrian boriban, care school), under OPEC | 223 | 28 April 2022 | Ministry of Education[^7] |

The two counts overlap but are not the same. Not every OPEC care school need hold DHSS accreditation, and DHSS-accredited institutions include universities and other bodies. We found no newer figure for either count.

The 2022 Ministry of Education meeting was held with the Care School Association, สมาคมโรงเรียนบริบาล (Samakhom Rongrian Boriban), and with DHSS, the Department of Employment, the Department of Consular Affairs, the Thailand Professional Qualification Institute and the Thailand Nursing and Midwifery Council. It aimed to link the caregiver course with occupational standards under the national qualifications framework.[^7]

## Other related courses

- **Nursing Council care-assistant certificate.** OPEC lists a *draft* certificate course for care assistants, พนักงานให้การดูแล (phanakngan hai kan dulae, care assistant), from the Thailand Nursing and Midwifery Council, สภาการพยาบาล (Sapha Kan Phayaban). Schools need the Council's approval before applying to use it.[^3]
- **Department of Skill Development courses.** OPEC says non-formal schools must be licensed by the Department of Skill Development, Ministry of Labour, before they can use its courses.[^3]
- **Thai Red Cross nurse aide.** The Srisavarindhira Thai Red Cross Institute of Nursing, สถาบันการพยาบาลศรีสวรินทิรา สภากาชาดไทย (Sathaban Kan Phayaban Si Sawarinthira Sapha Kachat Thai), is taking 110 students in 2026 for its nurse-aide certificate, ผู้ช่วยพยาบาล (phu chuai phayaban, practical nurse aide). It is a nurse-aide course, not a course designed for elderly care. For 2026 to 2027 it runs for one year, 10 August 2026 to 18 July 2027. Theory is taught **online** Monday to Thursday, 17:00 to 20:00, and at the institute on Fridays. Applicants are Thai nationals aged 16 to 35 who have finished upper-secondary school. The course is offered under a Thai Red Cross scholarship. Students pay a fee of 2,270 baht on registration day, and graduates must work at Red Cross units for at least twice the study period.[^8]

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: for a software trial, Thai caregiver schools are reachable and already digital in small ways. The 420-hour standard has an 18-hour language and computer module that names "applications". DHSS already makes accredited schools upload each cohort's graduates to its system. The Red Cross teaches theory online. A trial offer that fits this would be a free classroom copy of a care-record app built around the inspection items in the DHSS standards (the client register, the three-monthly reassessment, daily records), used in the 220 practical hours. Good first contacts: the Care School Association, which speaks for many of the 223 OPEC schools; one or two university operator-course providers such as Chulabhorn Royal Academy; and DHSS's regional Health Service Support Centres, which accredit courses. The school lists are hard to reach: the DHSS registry refused our connection and the counts are from 2022 and 2023. A first job is to get a current list, by asking DHSS or OPEC directly.
