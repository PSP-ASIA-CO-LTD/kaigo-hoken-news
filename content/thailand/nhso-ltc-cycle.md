*Last updated: 11 Oct 2026*

Thailand's main public scheme for older people who need long-term care at home is run by the National Health Security Office (NHSO), สำนักงานหลักประกันสุขภาพแห่งชาติ (Samnakngan Lak Prakan Sukkhaphap haeng Chat, abbreviated สปสช.), together with local governments. The NHSO set up the fund in 2016. It first covered only people aged 60 and over under the Universal Coverage scheme, and later widened to dependent people of every age and every health scheme.[^6] This guide follows one person through the cycle, from the first screening to the caregiver's pay and the yearly report. For the size of the budget, see [Who pays for long-term care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-pays-for-long-term-care" >}}). For the people involved, see [Who does what in Thai community care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-does-what-community-care" >}}). For the systems, see [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "/thailand/data-and-it-reporting" >}}).

A note on sources. The NHSO's own manuals and notices sit on government sites that refused our connections. This guide therefore relies on official texts republished by local governments (a Royal Gazette notice, a local fund's copy of the NHSO rate annex, an NHSO payment guideline, a sub-district's ltcnew manual), a Department of Health research report, and fact sheets from a public health-workforce research office. Each step names its source, and anything we could not confirm is in the "Not verified" list at the end.

## The cycle at a glance

| Step | What happens | Who | System or record | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | The local government joins the local health security fund and the LTC scheme | Local government | — | [^6] |
| 1 | Dependent people (old and new cases) are screened with the Barthel ADL index | Service unit (care manager) | Paper or local forms | [^1][^4][^5] |
| 2 | People scoring 11 or less are registered | Service unit; local government | NHSO LTC programme | [^4] |
| 3 | Assessment and an individual care plan, by care group | Care manager with the health team | Department of Health 3C program | [^3][^5][^7] |
| 4 | The plans go to the LTC subcommittee as a project for approval | LTC subcommittee | Printed care plans attached to a project | [^2][^3][^4] |
| 5 | The NHSO pays a lump sum to the local government; the local government pays the service unit | NHSO; local government | NHSO LTC programme (project, agreement, voucher, payment) | [^4] |
| 6 | Caregivers visit according to the plan, supervised by the care manager | Caregiver; care manager | Printed care plan | [^3][^7][^9] |
| 7 | Caregivers are paid by one of three routes | Service unit, local government or NHSO | NHSO LTC programme (stimulus route) | [^8][^9] |
| 8 | Twelve months of service and ADL are recorded, and the project reports its results | Service unit; local government | NHSO LTC programme | [^4] |

## Step 0: the local government joins

The NHSO pays the long-term care money through each area's local health security fund, กองทุนหลักประกันสุขภาพในระดับท้องถิ่นหรือพื้นที่ (kongthun lak prakan sukkhaphap nai radap thongthin rue phuenthi). A local government must already belong to that fund before it can join the long-term care scheme.[^6] A fact sheet based on a 2021 study counted about 6,800 of more than 7,700 local governments as members.[^6] The scheme expects the local government to add its own resources and staff.[^6]

Care plans are approved by a local subcommittee, usually shortened to "the LTC subcommittee", คณะอนุกรรมการ LTC (khana anukammakan LTC, LTC subcommittee). The 2019 NHSO notice gives its full name: the subcommittee supporting long-term care services for dependent older people, คณะอนุกรรมการสนับสนุนการจัดบริการดูแลระยะยาวสำหรับผู้สูงอายุที่มีภาวะพึ่งพิง (khana anukammakan sanapsanun kan chat borikan dulae raya yao samrap phu sung-ayu thi mi phawa phuengphing).[^2]

## Step 1: Barthel ADL screening

**The test.** Eligibility is set by the Barthel Activities of Daily Living (ADL) index, ดัชนีบาร์เธลเอดีแอล (datchani Barthel ADL). It scores ten items:[^1]

1. eating, when the food is set out
2. face washing, hair, teeth and shaving in the past 24 to 48 hours
3. getting from bed to chair
4. toilet use
5. moving around the room or house
6. dressing
7. climbing one flight of stairs
8. bathing
9. bowel control in the past week
10. bladder control in the past week

**Who qualifies.** A person with a score of 11 or less counts as a dependent person, ผู้ที่มีภาวะพึ่งพิง (phu thi mi phawa phuengphing). Scores of 0 to 4 form the bed-bound group, กลุ่มติดเตียง (klum tit tiang), and 5 to 11 the home-bound group, กลุ่มติดบ้าน (klum tit ban).[^1] Since a 2019 notice the rule applies to older people and "other persons" alike, which opened the scheme to younger dependent people.[^2] In 2024 the NHSO board added two more groups: people with dementia from the moderate stage, and people at the end of life.[^1]

**Who screens.** In the NHSO's 2023 workflow chart, the service unit, หน่วยจัดบริการ (nuai chat borikan), screens dependent people, both old and new cases.[^4] One municipal fund's 2016 guideline asks its service units to survey older people every July with the Barthel form and to summarise those scoring 11 or less into four groups on a form called LTC 1.[^5]

## Step 2: registration in the NHSO LTC programme

The people screened are registered in the NHSO's LTC programme.[^4] A fact sheet on care managers says it is the care manager who enters each person's details in the NHSO's long-term care database, for the claim.[^7] The 2023 workflow chart places registration between screening and the care plan.[^4]

The programme runs at ltcnew.nhso.go.th. Our [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "/thailand/data-and-it-reporting" >}}) guide describes what its public pages show.

## Step 3: assessment, care groups and the care plan

**Who plans.** The care manager's main duty is the individual care plan, covering home visits and the health services each person needs, and coordinating the other professions in the area.[^7]

**The four care groups.** Within the dependency scores, people are placed in four groups, กลุ่ม 1–4 (klum 1–4), according to their care needs.[^5] The fullest description we found is a 2016 rate annex republished by a municipal health fund:[^5]

| Group | Description in the 2016 annex | Care manager review | Family-doctor team visits | Caregiver visits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (home-bound) | Can move a little; may have eating or continence problems; no confusion | At least every 6 months | At least once a month | 2 to 4 a month |
| 2 (home-bound) | As group 1, but with confusion | At least every 3 months | At least once a month | Once a week (4 to 8 a month) |
| 3 (bed-bound) | Cannot move without help; may have eating or continence problems or serious illness | At least every 3 months | At least once a month | Once a week (4 to 12 a month) |
| 4 (bed-bound) | As group 3, and may be seriously ill or at the end of life | At least monthly | At least twice a month | Twice a week (8 to 12 a month) |

Source for the table: the 2016 annex.[^5] In the same annex every group gets one full assessment and plan a year.[^5] A 2025 NHSO guideline still uses numbered groups, including a sub-group "4.2, near death", in its example timetable of caregiver visits.[^9] We did not find the current official definitions of the groups or of the TAI classification that the NHSO programme also asks for (see Step 5).

**The care plan.** The care plan, แผนการดูแลรายบุคคล (phaen kan dulae rai bukkhon, individual care plan), sets out the care the person will receive from the family-doctor team and the caregiver.[^1] The 2016 annex expects one full assessment and plan a year for every group.[^5]

**Writing it in 3C.** The care manager writes the plan in the Department of Health's 3C program. The name stands for its three parts: care manager, caregiver and care plan. It registers care managers and caregivers and holds the care plans.[^3] The care manager pulls the list of dependent people from the NHSO LTC programme into 3C, writes the plan, and prints it, both for the LTC subcommittee and for the caregiver.[^3] The care-manager fact sheet describes the same duty from the other side: care plans and caregivers' work go into the Department of Health's database, and each person's details go into the NHSO's.[^7]

## Step 4: approval by the LTC subcommittee

The care manager prints the plans from 3C and submits them to the LTC subcommittee.[^3] In the 2023 workflow chart, the care plans are put forward as a project, the subcommittee considers and approves the project, and the approval is then recorded in the NHSO LTC programme.[^4] The legal basis is a 2019 NHSO board notice. It says the fund's money for dependent people goes to service providers "according to projects approved by" the LTC subcommittee.[^2]

The 2016 municipal guideline shows what this looks like locally:[^5]

- the service unit sends its proposal (form LTC 3) with the care plans (form LTC 2) to the subcommittee by August each year
- the subcommittee summarises the proposals (LTC 4), meets, and records its decision (LTC 4/1 and LTC 5) as the evidence for the mayor to order payment
- if a proposal is rejected, the unit is told why and may resubmit
- approved proposals are paid within 15 working days of the decision

This is one fund's practice from 2016, not a national timetable.

## Step 5: the money

**From the NHSO to the local government.** In the 2023 workflow chart, once the approval is recorded, the NHSO head office pulls the data and processes the payment, the payment data are confirmed, the NHSO regional office checks the payment conditions, and the lump sum is transferred to the local government. The local government then draws up the agreement and voucher and transfers the money to the service unit.[^4]

**The amount.** The NHSO board approved 10,442 baht per dependent person per year, up from 6,000 baht, announced in June 2024.[^1] The board expected about 600,000 people to be covered, against 320,000 before.[^1] The NHSO also paid an "on top" amount to the service units: 100,000 baht for each unit in the integrated scheme, for screening, ADL assessment and care planning, according to a fact sheet describing the 2019 budget.[^6]

**In the NHSO LTC programme.** A sub-district's manual for the programme lists the local government's tasks, with the menu names:[^4]

1. import the project (บริหารโครงการ › นำเข้าโครงการ): name the service unit, enter the date the LTC subcommittee approved the care plans, then add each approved care plan, entering the person's **ADL score, care group and TAI**
2. record the project approval (บันทึกอนุมัติโครงการ) and confirm it
3. draw up the agreement (จัดทำข้อตกลง) and print two copies
4. issue the payment voucher, ฎีกาเบิกจ่าย (dika boek chai)
5. record the payment method (บันทึกจ่ายเงิน), and transfer the money to the unit responsible for the project, with a receipt
6. when the project ends, the unit responsible reports its results

**Who receives the money.** The 2019 notice names three kinds of recipient: community elderly quality-of-life development centres, service units, and service facilities.[^2] See [Who does what in Thai community care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-does-what-community-care" >}}) for each one.

### The 10,442 baht and the care groups: is there a rule?

We looked for an official rule that ties the per-person amount to the care groups. Here is what we found.

1. **In 2016, yes.** The rate annex republished by a municipal fund sets a yearly compensation range for each group: 4,000 baht for group 1, 3,000 to 6,000 for group 2, 4,000 to 8,000 for group 3, and 5,000 to 10,000 for group 4. It also caps the care manager's assessment and planning at 800 baht per person per year, and pays caregivers 50 baht per visit of at least one hour.[^5] The fund itself received a flat amount per registered person; the caregiver fact sheet gives it as 5,000 baht a year.[^8]
2. **Later, 6,000 baht.** A fact sheet on the NHSO's fund gives 6,000 baht per head in its text. The diagram in the same fact sheet, adapted from the 2019 fund manual, shows 5,000.[^6] The 2023 workflow chart shows 6,000.[^4]
3. **Since FY2024, a flat 10,442 baht.** The 2024 announcement gives one amount per dependent person per year. It mentions the bed-bound and home-bound groups only as part of the eligibility rule, not as payment bands.[^1] The NHSO's LTC programme site, quoting the FY2024 fund notice, describes the payment as 10,442 baht per dependent person per year against an approved individual care plan (see [Who pays for long-term care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-pays-for-long-term-care" >}})).
4. **The programme still records the group.** Each care plan entered by the local government must carry the person's ADL score, group and TAI.[^4]

**Conclusion.** We found no current published rule that splits the 10,442 baht by care group. The national amount is a flat lump sum per approved care plan. How a local subcommittee divides the money between people in different groups is not set in any source we opened. The 2016 per-group ranges are the only official banding we found, and we could not confirm whether they still apply. We could not open the FY2024 fund notice itself, so this is listed as not verified.

## Step 6: caregiver visits and records

The caregiver gives the care set out in the plan, and the care manager is responsible for supervising that work.[^7] Most of the care is help with daily living: bathing, eating, dressing, toileting and moving about.[^8]

**How often.** The 2016 annex sets visit frequencies by group (see the table in Step 3).[^5] The 2025 NHSO guideline gives an example timetable with a standard number of visits and hours for each group. It cites a 2022 study of the scheme's financing and the Department of Health as its sources:[^9]

| Example person | Group | Visits | Standard time per visit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | 2 a month | 2 hours |
| 2 | 2 | 1 a week | 4 hours |
| 3 | 3 | 1 a week | 4 hours |
| 4 | 4.2 (near death) | 2 a week | 4 hours |

Source for the table: the 2025 NHSO guideline, table 1.[^9]

**Records.** The caregiver works from the care plan printed from 3C.[^3] The care manager enters the caregivers' work in the Department of Health's database.[^7] The 2023 workflow chart ends with a record of ADL for 12 months of service in the NHSO programme.[^4] Under the 2025 stimulus project, each caregiver also sends a monthly service report to the local government.[^9] We found no national form or app for the caregiver's own visit log in the NHSO scheme.

## Step 7: paying caregivers

There are three routes, and an area may use more than one.

**1. The LTC fund through a service unit.** Where the local government signs an agreement with a local health facility, usually a sub-district health promoting hospital, to run the scheme, the caregiver is paid at the fund's rate. A 2021 study found 600 baht a month for a caregiver with 1 to 5 cases, and 1,500 baht for 6 to 10 cases.[^8] Where the local government runs its own community elderly quality-of-life development centre, it may set its own rate, up to the local minimum wage.[^8] The same study found that areas with their own centre paid caregivers more and gave more continuous care.[^8]

**2. Local care volunteers hired by the local government.** In 2019 the Ministry of Interior told every local government to hire at least two full-time caregivers, called "volunteer caregivers", to support the volunteer caregivers already in the scheme. They must have the Department of Health's 70-hour caregiver course. They are paid 5,000 baht a month, or 6,000 baht with 50 more hours of training. Local governments were to fill the posts by FY2020.[^8] The 2025 guideline refers to the Ministry of Interior's regulation on local care volunteers, อาสาสมัครบริบาลท้องถิ่น (asasamak boriban thongthin, local care volunteer).[^9]

**3. The 2025 economic-stimulus project, paid by the NHSO directly.** The cabinet approved a project for dependent people in the community on 24 June 2025 and named the NHSO to run it. The NHSO board approved the guidelines on 7 July 2025.[^9] The guideline says:[^9]

- the money is outside the NHSO's normal fund for dependent people, and local governments do not co-fund it
- sub-district administrative organisations, องค์การบริหารส่วนตำบล (ongkan borihan suan tambon, abbreviated อบต.), municipalities, เทศบาล (thetsaban), Bangkok and Pattaya may take part
- contracts had to be signed by 30 September 2025, for one year; the project ran from 7 July 2025 to 30 September 2026
- targets: 18,587 caregivers hired and 106,807 dependent people cared for
- pay: up to 5,000 baht a month (60,000 a year) for the Department of Health's 70-hour course or an equivalent course recognised by a state agency, and up to 6,000 baht a month (72,000 a year) for more than 70 hours
- caregivers may not be civil servants, state employees or others with regular official pay
- each caregiver opens an account at the Bank for Agriculture and Agricultural Cooperatives, ธ.ก.ส. (Tho Ko So, BAAC)

The steps run through the NHSO LTC programme:[^9]

1. the local government and the care manager survey dependent people and find those without a caregiver
2. the local government appoints the caregiver and signs the contract
3. the local government uploads to ltcnew a request letter that lets the NHSO pay the caregiver on its behalf, the contract, the bank-book page, the training certificate and the ID numbers of the people assigned; the NHSO regional office checks the contract
4. the caregiver works to the care plan under the care manager and reports monthly to the local government
5. the local government accepts the month's work and records it in ltcnew
6. the NHSO pays the caregiver each month
7. the NHSO and the local government audit the results

Our [Who pays for long-term care]({{< relref "/thailand/who-pays-for-long-term-care" >}}) guide gives the NHSO's running tally of caregivers hired under this project.

## Step 8: reporting and the next round

The 2023 workflow chart ends with ADL recorded over 12 months of service.[^4] The sub-district manual adds that, when the project ends, the unit responsible reports its results.[^4] The 2016 municipal guideline repeats the cycle each year: a new survey every July and new proposals by August.[^5]

## Which system does what

| Task | NHSO LTC programme (ltcnew) | Department of Health 3C program |
|---|---|---|
| Register dependent people for funding | Yes[^4][^7] | Pulls the list from the NHSO programme[^3] |
| Register care managers and caregivers | Caregivers hired under the 2025 stimulus project[^9] | Yes[^3] |
| Write the care plan | Records each approved plan's ADL score, group and TAI[^4] | Yes; printed for the subcommittee and the caregiver[^3] |
| Approve projects and amounts | Local government records the subcommittee's approval[^4] | No |
| Pay | Agreement, voucher and payment record; stimulus-project pay[^4][^9] | No |
| Report | 12-month ADL record; project results; stimulus reports[^4][^9] | Caregivers' work (entered by the care manager)[^7] |
| Built by | NHSO | Department of Health with Maejo University[^3] |

The Department of Health and the NHSO agreed in November 2018 to link the two systems' data.[^3] Even so, a 2021 study found that the lack of a link between the Department of Health's care-plan system and the NHSO's system made care managers do the same paperwork twice.[^7] Our [Data and IT reporting]({{< relref "/thailand/data-and-it-reporting" >}}) guide covers the planned "3C PLUS" link.

{{< analysis title="Tako-San's take" >}}
Tako-San's view: on paper the Thai cycle is close to Japan's care-management cycle: assess, plan, approve, deliver, review. In practice it is split across two government systems and a lot of paper. The care manager screens and registers in the NHSO programme, plans in 3C, prints the plan for a local committee, and then the local government re-enters the approved plans to get paid. Caregivers have no system of their own in the main scheme. Three things stand out for anyone who builds care software. First, the national money is now a flat amount per approved plan, but the programme still asks for the care group on every plan, and the only official banding we found is from 2016. Any tool that "calculates" a Thai care-plan budget by group should say which rule it uses. Second, the problems the government's own researchers name are double entry between the two systems and care managers who are nurses with other jobs. Those are workflow problems, not shortages of features. Third, the stimulus route shows the NHSO can pay individual caregivers monthly against records in its own programme. Whether that continues after 30 September 2026 decides how much monthly caregiver activity has to be recorded.
{{< /analysis >}}

## Not verified

- **The FY2024 fund notice itself.** We rely on the 2024 board announcement and the NHSO programme site's quotation of it. The notice's text, and whether it contains any banding by group, were not opened.
- **Whether the 2016 per-group ranges still apply** in any form, or were withdrawn when the lump sum became flat.
- **Current official definitions of the four care groups and of TAI.** The group descriptions above are from a 2016 annex. The TAI classification is required by the programme, but we did not find its current definition.
- **National deadlines.** The August submission and 15-day payment rule are one municipal fund's 2016 guideline. We found no current national calendar.
- **Whether the stimulus project continues** after 30 September 2026. We found no announcement either way.
- **Caregiver visit records.** We found no national form or app for the caregiver's own visit log in the NHSO scheme.
- **Details of the 3C program.** The Department of Health's 3C report could not be reopened while this guide was written (the site refused our connections), so only its basic workflow is used here. Its survey figures for health region 8 are left out until they can be checked.
